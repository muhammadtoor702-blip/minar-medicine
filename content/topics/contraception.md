## Overview

Contraception is one of the most impactful areas of preventive medicine — access to effective contraception is a fundamental determinant of maternal health, reducing both unintended pregnancy and the morbidity of unsafe abortion. Approximately 45% of all pregnancies worldwide are unintended. The UK Faculty of Sexual and Reproductive Healthcare (FSRH) and UK Medical Eligibility Criteria (UKMEC) provide the evidence framework for safe prescribing. UKMEC classifies conditions into four categories: UKMEC 1 (no restriction), UKMEC 2 (benefits outweigh risks), UKMEC 3 (risks generally outweigh benefits — use only if alternatives unavailable), UKMEC 4 (absolute contraindication — unacceptable health risk).

## Pathophysiology

### Mechanisms of Action by Method

**Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COCP):**
- Oestrogen (ethinylestradiol) + progestogen (various: levonorgestrel, norethindrone, desogestrel, drospirenone)
- **Primary mechanism:** Suppresses GnRH → inhibits FSH and LH surge → prevents ovulation
- **Secondary mechanisms:** Progestogen thickens cervical mucus (impairs sperm penetration); oestrogen + progestogen thin endometrium (reduces implantation risk as backup)
- Pearl Index: 0.3% with perfect use; 9% with typical use

**Progestogen-Only Pill (POP):**
- Traditional POP (norethindrone, levonorgestrel): primarily cervical mucus thickening + endometrial atrophy; ovulation inhibition unreliable — must be taken within 3-hour window
- **Desogestrel POP (Cerazette/Cerelle):** Reliably inhibits ovulation in >97% of cycles + cervical mucus; 12-hour window → preferred POP; suitable where oestrogen contraindicated
- Pearl Index: 0.3–1% with perfect use

**Intrauterine Devices:**
- **Copper IUD (non-hormonal):** Copper ions are directly spermicidal; also impairs fertilisation + causes endometrial inflammatory reaction → reduces implantation; no systemic effects; most effective form of emergency contraception
- **Levonorgestrel-releasing IUS (Mirena 52 mg; Kyleena 19.5 mg; Jaydess 13.5 mg):** Local progestogen → endometrial atrophy + cervical mucus thickening; systemic absorption minimal; Mirena lasts 8 years (licensed for 5 years in UK but NICE 2019 extended to 8); Pearl Index <0.2%

**Implant (Nexplanon):**
- Etonogestrel rod inserted subdermally in upper arm; lasts 3 years
- Inhibits ovulation reliably; most effective reversible contraceptive (Pearl Index 0.05%)
- Systemic progestogen: irregular bleeding most common side effect

**Injectable (Depo-Provera — medroxyprogesterone acetate 150 mg IM every 12 weeks):**
- Reliable ovulation suppression; highly effective
- **Return to fertility may be delayed by 1–2 years** after stopping — important counselling point
- Bone density reduction with prolonged use (DEXA monitoring if used >2 years)

**Barrier methods:** Male condom (98% perfect use, 85% typical), female condom (95% perfect use), diaphragm (92–96%). Only method providing significant STI protection — dual protection (condom + hormonal) recommended when STI risk present.

## Clinical Presentation

### UKMEC Contraindications — Key Categories

**UKMEC 4 (Absolute contraindications to COCP):**
- Migraine WITH aura (any age) — oestrogen increases stroke risk in aura-associated cortical spreading depression
- Current or history of VTE (DVT/PE)
- Known thrombogenic mutation (factor V Leiden, prothrombin gene mutation)
- History of ischaemic heart disease or stroke
- Uncontrolled hypertension (SBP ≥160 mmHg or DBP ≥100 mmHg)
- Active liver disease (hepatitis, cirrhosis, liver tumour)
- Breast cancer (current)
- Smoking ≥15 cigarettes/day + age ≥35 years

**UKMEC 3 (Relative contraindication — risks generally outweigh benefits for COCP):**
- Smoking <15 cigarettes/day + age ≥35 years
- BMI ≥35 kg/m² (VTE risk)
- Hypertension (BP adequately controlled — systolic 140–159 or diastolic 90–99)
- Migraine WITHOUT aura, age <35 (UKMEC 2 if no aura; UKMEC 4 if with aura)
- Past breast cancer (≥5 years remission)
- Diabetes with vascular disease

**Progestogen-only methods (POP, IUS, implant, injection) — safer in:**
- Migraine with aura — UKMEC 2 for POP/implant
- Hypertension — UKMEC 2 for POP/implant (no oestrogen → no additional VTE risk)
- Smoking — UKMEC 2
- BMI ≥35 — UKMEC 2

### Drug Interactions — Enzyme Inducers

**Enzyme-inducing drugs** (rifampicin, some antiepileptics — phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, topiramate, oxcarbazepine) accelerate hepatic metabolism of oestrogen and progestogen → **reduced contraceptive efficacy**:
- COCP: use additional contraception OR switch to non-hormonal/intrauterine method
- **Implant/POP/injection: unreliable efficacy with enzyme inducers** — copper IUD or LNG-IUS strongly preferred
- **Lamotrigine is NOT an enzyme inducer** — but COCP induces lamotrigine metabolism → reduces lamotrigine levels by up to 50% → seizure risk; use non-oestrogen-containing method where possible OR monitor lamotrigine levels carefully

## Diagnosis

### Contraception Consultation Framework

**FSRH "Quick Start" criteria:** Contraception can be started at any point in the menstrual cycle if pregnancy is reasonably excluded (last menstrual period <5 days ago; abstinent/correct contraception use since last LMP; within 5 days of unprotected sex and emergency contraception has been given; breastfeeding amenorrhoea criteria met).

**Ruling out pregnancy before starting:** urine hCG if any risk. Implant and IUS insertion can proceed on the same day as emergency contraception.

### Efficacy Comparison (Pearl Index — Typical Use)

| Method | Failure Rate (Typical Use) | Duration |
|--------|---------------------------|----------|
| Implant (Nexplanon) | **0.05%** | 3 years |
| LNG-IUS (Mirena) | **<0.2%** | 8 years |
| Copper IUD | 0.8% | 10 years |
| COCP | 9% | Daily |
| POP | 9% | Daily (3h window, or 12h for desogestrel) |
| Male condom | 15% | Per act |
| Diaphragm | 12% | Per act |
| No contraception | 85% | — |

Long-acting reversible contraception (LARC) — implant, IUS, IUD, injection — have significantly higher real-world efficacy than user-dependent methods.

## Management

### Emergency Contraception

Three options, in increasing efficacy:

**1. Levonorgestrel (Levonelle) 1.5 mg orally:**
- Within **72 hours** (3 days) of unprotected sex — efficacy decreases with time
- Mechanism: Delays/inhibits ovulation; not effective if ovulation already occurred
- Available OTC; no prescription required
- Reduces pregnancy risk by ~85% if taken within 72h
- **Not effective in women >70 kg** — use ulipristal or double dose (controversial)

**2. Ulipristal acetate (ellaOne) 30 mg orally:**
- Within **120 hours** (5 days) of unprotected sex
- Selective progesterone receptor modulator → delays/inhibits ovulation even closer to LH surge than levonorgestrel → more effective
- Efficacy maintained to 120h (unlike levonorgestrel which declines after 72h)
- **Cannot breastfeed for 1 week after**
- **Not with enzyme-inducing drugs** (metabolism accelerated)
- Prescription required (or direct-access pharmacy schemes)
- Do not use if breastfeeding (use LNG instead)

**3. Copper IUD:**
- Within **120 hours** of unprotected sex — **most effective EC (>99.9%)**
- Also effective if inserted within 5 days of the earliest expected ovulation (extends window in longer cycles)
- Mechanism: spermicidal + prevents fertilisation/implantation
- Can be left in situ as ongoing long-term contraception (10 years)
- Preferred option in women >70 kg, when ulipristal is contraindicated, or when ongoing LARC is desired

### COCP — Prescribing and Starting

- **Day 1 start:** Begin day 1 of menstrual cycle → immediately effective; no additional contraception needed
- **Quick start (any cycle day):** Additional contraception (condom) for 7 days; pregnancy test in 3 weeks
- **21-day pack:** 21 active pills, 7-day pill-free interval (withdrawal bleed during pill-free interval — not a true menstruation)
- **Missed pill rules:** 1 pill missed → take as soon as remembered, no extra precautions; 2+ consecutive pills missed in week 1 → emergency contraception if sex in pill-free interval or week 1

### Specific Situations

**Breastfeeding:** COCP contraindicated (oestrogen reduces milk supply) — use progestogen-only methods (POP, implant, IUS, injection) from 6 weeks postpartum.

**Perimenopause:** Contraception required until 12 months after final LMP if >50 years, or 24 months if <50 years. LNG-IUS provides contraception + endometrial protection if adding HRT oestrogen.

**Post-abortion/miscarriage:** All methods can be started immediately post-procedure.

## Complications

- **VTE:** COCP increases VTE risk 3–4× baseline (from ~2 to ~6–8 per 10,000 woman-years); third-generation progestogens (desogestrel, gestodene) carry slightly higher risk than levonorgestrel-containing pills; transdermal patch highest VTE risk
- **Arterial thrombosis/stroke:** Oestrogen + migraine with aura → synergistic stroke risk (UKMEC 4)
- **Breast cancer:** Small increased relative risk with COCP (RCGP Oral Contraception Study: RR ~1.1–1.3); risk returns to baseline within 10 years of stopping
- **Cervical cancer:** Prolonged COCP use (>5 years) slightly increases cervical cancer risk — may be confounded by reduced condom use (HPV exposure)
- **Ectopic pregnancy risk with POP:** If POP fails (rare), higher proportion of failures are ectopic — important counselling point
- **Irregular bleeding (progestogen methods):** Implant/POP cause irregular/absent periods — counsel before insertion; does not indicate failure
- **Copper IUD — increased menorrhagia and dysmenorrhoea:** Significant enough to cause removal in ~10% — must counsel; treat with mefenamic acid/tranexamic acid
- **IUS/IUD insertion complications:** Perforation (1–2 per 1000 insertions), expulsion (5% per year), PID (mainly in first 3 weeks — screen for Chlamydia/gonorrhoea before insertion)

## Clinical Insight

The COCP and migraine with aura represents one of the clearest absolute contraindications in reproductive medicine, yet it is frequently missed in practice. The mechanism is not simply about "blood clotting" — cortical spreading depression (the neurophysiological basis of migraine aura) causes transient oligaemia in the cortex. Oestrogen independently increases cerebrovascular risk through effects on coagulation, endothelial function, and vasomotor tone. The combination creates a substantially elevated ischaemic stroke risk — UKMEC 4 regardless of age, regardless of whether hypertension is controlled, regardless of whether the patient has been on the pill for years without problems. The correct action is always to switch to a progestogen-only or non-hormonal method.

The "pill" is often blamed as a cause of reduced libido, mood changes, and weight gain. The evidence is more nuanced: while some women clearly experience these effects — particularly with older, higher-dose formulations — high-quality RCT data do not consistently demonstrate an effect on weight. Mood effects may be real, particularly in women with pre-existing depression. The clinical take is to take the patient's concerns seriously, try a different preparation, or switch to a non-hormonal method — not to dismiss the symptoms.

LARC methods (implant, IUS, IUD) are consistently underused relative to their efficacy. The implant has an annual failure rate of approximately 0.05% — roughly 170 times more effective than typical use of the COCP. The correct framing for patient counselling is: LARC methods remove the human error factor entirely. For the scenario patient (BMI 34, hypertension, migraine with aura, on lamotrigine), the **copper IUD is the ideal choice** — non-hormonal (no VTE risk, no drug interactions), immediately effective, provides EC for the current incident, and offers 10 years of protection.

## Scenario Walkthrough

**The patient:** 28F, BMI 34, migraine with aura, smoker 10/day, BP 148/92, on lamotrigine, unprotected sex 48 hours ago. Wants most effective LARC and emergency contraception.

**Emergency contraception (immediate priority — 48 hours post-UPS):**
- Levonorgestrel 72h window: still within window but less effective at 48h
- Ulipristal: 120h window, more effective at 48h
- **Copper IUD: best option — >99.9% EC efficacy, can be retained as ongoing LARC**
- This patient's BMI 34 (>70 kg): levonorgestrel less effective → copper IUD or ulipristal preferred
- **Plan: Insert copper IUD today → EC + ongoing 10-year contraception in one procedure**

**Ongoing contraception assessment:**
- COCP: UKMEC 4 for migraine with aura AND UKMEC 3 for hypertension AND UKMEC 3 for smoking + BMI 34 → **absolutely contraindicated**
- LNG-IUS (Mirena): progestogen-only → UKMEC 2 for all her conditions → safe; excellent efficacy
- Implant: UKMEC 2 for her conditions BUT lamotrigine is NOT an enzyme inducer (safe), BUT she wants to avoid hormonal methods → copper IUD preferred
- **Lamotrigine interaction:** COCP reduces lamotrigine levels by ~50% → seizure risk — another reason COCP is wrong here; copper IUD has zero drug interaction

**Final plan:**
- Insert copper IUD today as EC + ongoing contraception
- Discuss LNG-IUS as alternative if copper IUD causes heavy periods
- BP management: refer for hypertension assessment — BP 148/92 warrants treatment
- Smoking cessation counselling
- STI screen (Chlamydia/gonorrhoea) before/at IUD insertion

**Exam trigger:** *Migraine with aura + request for COCP* → UKMEC 4 — absolutely contraindicated; switch to progestogen-only or non-hormonal method. The distractor is prescribing COCP because "she's only 28 and has no other risk factors" — aura alone is an absolute contraindication at any age.
