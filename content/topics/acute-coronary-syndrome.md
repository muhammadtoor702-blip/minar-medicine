## Pathophysiology

ACS is a spectrum of conditions unified by a common mechanism: **rupture or erosion of a vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque** → platelet aggregation → thrombus formation → partial or complete coronary occlusion.

From Pathoma: The process begins with endothelial injury → LDL infiltration → macrophage recruitment → foam cell formation → fatty streak → fibrous plaque with a lipid core. The fibrous cap is vulnerable to rupture, particularly at its shoulder region.

**Type I vs Type II NSTEMI** (Inner Circle):
- **Type I** — acute plaque rupture with thrombus (true ACS)
- **Type II** — supply-demand mismatch without rupture (tachyarrhythmia, severe anaemia, sepsis, vasospasm) — different management, treat the cause

**Time is muscle:** Irreversible necrosis begins within 20–40 minutes of complete occlusion. Necrosis expands as a wavefront from subendocardium → epicardium.

## Clinical Reasoning — The Differential

**Classic presentation:** Central chest pressure/tightness/heaviness, radiation to jaw/left arm, diaphoresis, nausea. Maximum at onset or builds over minutes.

**Atypical presentations** (more common in women, elderly, diabetics — always consider):
- Dyspnoea without chest pain
- Epigastric pain (especially inferior MI — always do ECG)
- Jaw or arm pain alone
- Sudden fatigue or presyncope

**Must not miss differentials:**
- **Aortic dissection** — tearing pain radiating to back, maximal at onset, BP asymmetry between arms. **Never thrombolyse without excluding this**
- **PE** — pleuritic chest pain, dyspnoea, risk factors for DVT
- **Pericarditis** — sharp, positional, friction rub, diffuse saddle ST elevation + PR depression
- **Oesophageal rupture (Boerhaave)** — after vomiting, severe tearing pain, surgical emergency

**RV Infarct (Inner Circle):** Proximal RCA occlusion → inferior STEMI + RV failure. Classic triad: **hypotension + clear lungs + raised JVP**. Avoid nitrates (preload-dependent). Give IV fluids. Watch for AV block (RCA supplies AV node).

## Workup

**12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival** — the single most time-critical investigation. Don't delay for anything.

**STEMI criteria:**
- ST elevation ≥1mm in ≥2 contiguous limb leads, or ≥2mm in V2–V3 (≥2.5mm in men <40, ≥1.5mm in women)
- New LBBB with clinical ACS picture

**High-sensitivity Troponin (hsTn):**
- Rises within 1–3 hours of injury
- 0h + 3h protocol (some centres use 0h + 1h)
- Negative at 0h + low clinical probability = effectively rules out NSTEMI
- Single negative does NOT rule out UA

**CXR:** Pulmonary oedema (Killip class), cardiomegaly, mediastinal widening (aortic dissection)

**Bloods:** FBC, U&E, glucose, lipids, coagulation, blood group & save

**Echo (TTE):** Wall motion abnormalities confirm ischaemia; assess EF; detect mechanical complications

## Management

### All ACS — Immediate

- **Aspirin 300mg chewed** (loading dose)
- **P2Y12 inhibitor** — Ticagrelor 180mg preferred (PLATO trial: superior to clopidogrel) or Prasugrel (avoid if prior stroke/TIA, age >75, weight <60kg)
- **Anticoagulation** — LMWH (enoxaparin 1mg/kg SC BD) or UFH
- **O₂** only if SpO₂ <90%
- **GTN** for persistent pain if not hypotensive (avoid in RV infarct, hypotension, recent PDE5 inhibitor use)
- **Beta-blocker** — reduces O₂ demand. Contraindicated in cardiogenic shock, bradycardia, acute decompensated HF
- **Statin** — high-intensity (atorvastatin 80mg) as early as possible
- **Morphine** — for uncontrolled pain, use cautiously (may delay P2Y12 absorption)

**Thrombolysis contraindications:** Prior haemorrhagic stroke ever, ischaemic stroke <3 months, aortic dissection, active bleeding (non-menstrual), BP >180/110 uncontrolled, recent major surgery/trauma <3 weeks.

**Post-stent DAPT:** Minimum 12 months (aspirin + ticagrelor/clopidogrel)

### NSTEMI/Unstable Angina — Risk-Stratify

**TIMI Risk Score** (0–7 points — age, risk factors, known CAD, ST changes, troponin, severe angina, aspirin use):
- High risk (score ≥3) → early invasive strategy (angiography within 24h)
- Low risk (score 0–2) → conservative approach, non-invasive testing

**Immediate PCI** for NSTEMI if: haemodynamic instability, cardiogenic shock, severe LV dysfunction, life-threatening arrhythmia, refractory chest pain.

### Post-MI Secondary Prevention (Inner Circle)

| Medication | Indication |
|-----------|-----------|
| Dual antiplatelet (aspirin + P2Y12) | All patients — aspirin indefinitely, P2Y12 for 12 months |
| Beta-blocker | All patients, continued indefinitely |
| High-intensity statin | All patients, regardless of baseline LDL |
| ACE inhibitor/ARB | All patients, especially if LVEF <40%, HF, or diabetes |
| Aldosterone antagonist | EF <40% + HF symptoms or diabetes |

## High Yield Pearls

- Always get ECG within 10 minutes — it is the single most important and time-critical investigation
- Never thrombolyse before excluding aortic dissection — it is fatal
- RV infarct: inferior STEMI + hypotension + clear lungs + raised JVP → avoid nitrates, give fluids
- Type II NSTEMI: treat the underlying cause (anaemia, arrhythmia, sepsis) — PCI won't help
- Post-MI, ALL patients get: DAPT + beta-blocker + high-intensity statin + ACEi
- New conduction defect in IE = perivalvular abscess; in inferior MI = transient (RCA supplies AV node)
