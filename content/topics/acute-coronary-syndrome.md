## Overview

Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) is a spectrum of conditions unified by a common pathological mechanism: rupture or erosion of a vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque, followed by platelet aggregation, thrombus formation, and acute coronary occlusion. The three subtypes differ in the completeness of occlusion and the extent of myocardial injury.

| Feature | Unstable Angina | NSTEMI | STEMI |
|---------|----------------|--------|-------|
| Occlusion | Partial | Partial | Complete |
| Troponin | Normal | Elevated | Elevated |
| ST on ECG | No elevation (may depress) | No elevation | Elevation present |
| Immediate treatment | Medical ± angiography | Medical ± angiography | **Immediate reperfusion** |

ACS carries a substantial mortality: 50% of deaths occur within 2 hours of symptom onset. Up to 7% die before hospital discharge. Worse prognosis with older age, LV failure, and extensive ECG changes.

**Incidence:** >100,000 admissions per year in the UK for MI.

## Pathophysiology

The process begins years before the acute event. LDL infiltrates the arterial intima, is oxidised, and engulfed by macrophages to form foam cells — the cellular basis of the fatty streak. Progressive accumulation forms a fibrous plaque with a lipid-rich necrotic core, capped by a fibrous layer. The fibrous cap, particularly at its shoulder regions, is vulnerable to rupture under haemodynamic shear stress.

Rupture exposes subendothelial collagen and tissue factor → platelet adhesion, activation, and aggregation → coagulation cascade activation → thrombus formation. In STEMI, the thrombus completely occludes the artery. In NSTEMI/UA, it is occlusive but with incomplete or temporary occlusion.

**Irreversible myocardial necrosis begins within 20–40 minutes of complete occlusion** and progresses as a wavefront from the subendocardium outward toward the epicardium. Time is muscle — every minute without reperfusion increases final infarct size.

**Troponin types — clinically important distinction:**

| Type | Mechanism | Cause |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Type 1 | Plaque rupture/erosion with atherothrombosis | "True" ACS |
| Type 2 | Supply-demand mismatch unrelated to acute plaque | Tachyarrhythmia, severe anaemia, shock, respiratory failure, coronary spasm |
| Type 3 | Cardiac death; ECG suggesting MI, no troponin measured | |
| Type 4 | PCI-associated | |
| Type 5 | CABG-associated | |

Type 2 MI requires treatment of the underlying cause, not emergency angiography.

**ECG localisation of territory:**

| Territory | Culprit Vessel | ECG Leads |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Inferior | RCA (90%) or LCx | II, III, aVF |
| Anteroseptal | Proximal LAD | V1–V2 |
| Anteroapical | Mid LAD | V3–V4 |
| Anterolateral | LAD or LCx | I, aVL, V5–V6 |
| Posterior | PDA | V1–V3 ST depression, dominant R (do posterior leads V7–V9) |
| Right ventricular | Proximal RCA | V4R (right-sided leads) |

## Clinical Presentation

**Typical presentation:** Central chest pressure, tightness, or heaviness — often described as a weight, band, or vice. Radiation to the left arm, jaw, or neck. Associated diaphoresis, nausea, vomiting, and dyspnoea. Pain builds over minutes and lasts >20 minutes (unlike stable angina, which resolves with rest in <5 minutes).

**Atypical presentations** are more common in women, elderly patients, and diabetics: isolated dyspnoea, epigastric pain and vomiting, jaw or arm pain alone, unexplained presyncope, confusion. A normal-feeling presentation does not exclude ACS. Silent MIs may present as: syncope, pulmonary oedema, post-operative hypotension, acute confusional state, or hyperglycaemic states in diabetics.

**Signs:** Distress, pallor, diaphoresis. Pulse normal, rapid, or slow (inferior MI → bradycardia from vagal activation). BP may be high (pain/catecholamines) or low (pump failure or RV infarction). 4th heart sound. Signs of heart failure: raised JVP, 3rd heart sound, basal crepitations. Low-grade fever after 24 hours. A new pansystolic murmur suggests papillary muscle dysfunction or rupture.

**Right ventricular infarction** (proximal RCA occlusion, inferior STEMI): presents with the triad of hypotension, elevated JVP, and clear lung fields. These patients are **preload-dependent** — nitrates and diuretics are dangerous and may cause cardiovascular collapse. Treatment is IV fluid resuscitation.

**Must-not-miss differentials:**
- **Aortic dissection** — tearing pain maximal at onset, interscapular radiation, BP asymmetry between arms, widened mediastinum. Thrombolysis in this setting is fatal.
- **PE** — pleuritic pain, dyspnoea, risk factors for DVT, haemoptysis, tachycardia
- **Pericarditis** — sharp positional pain, friction rub, diffuse saddle-shaped ST elevation with PR depression

## Diagnosis

**ECG within 10 minutes of arrival** — the single most time-critical investigation. Do not delay for anything.

**STEMI** is defined by:
- ST elevation ≥1mm in ≥2 contiguous limb leads, or
- ST elevation ≥2mm in V2–V3 (≥2.5mm in men under 40; ≥1.5mm in women), or
- New LBBB in a clinical context consistent with ACS, or
- ST elevation in posterior leads V7–V9 (posterior MI)

Importantly, paced rhythms and pre-existing LBBB may obscure STEMI — use modified Sgarbossa criteria and clinical judgement.

**High-sensitivity troponin:** Rises within 1–3 hours of myocardial injury. Serial testing using a validated high-sensitivity assay (0h/1h or 0h/3h protocol) can effectively rule in or rule out NSTEMI. A single negative troponin at presentation does not exclude unstable angina (troponin-negative ACS). In CKD and dialysis patients, troponin is chronically elevated — the **change** in level is more important than the absolute value.

**CXR:** Cardiomegaly, pulmonary oedema, widened mediastinum (raises concern for dissection). Do not delay treatment routinely while awaiting CXR.

**Blood tests:** FBC, U&E, glucose, lipids, cardiac enzymes. Elevated glucose on admission is a negative prognostic sign even in non-diabetics.

**Echo:** Regional wall motion abnormalities confirm MI territory; assesses LV function (EF drives subsequent treatment decisions); identifies mechanical complications (MR, VSD, pericardial effusion).

**GRACE score:** Validated risk score for NSTEMI; incorporates age, HR, systolic BP, creatinine, Killip class, cardiac arrest, elevated troponin, ST deviation. Score >140 = high risk → angiography within 24 hours.

## Management

### Immediate — All ACS

- **Aspirin 300mg** chewed immediately (loading dose)
- **P2Y12 inhibitor** — ticagrelor 180mg loading dose preferred over clopidogrel (PLATO trial: superior outcomes, regardless of revascularisation strategy). Prasugrel 60mg for STEMI patients proceeding to PCI (contraindicated if prior stroke/TIA, age >75, weight <60kg). Clopidogrel 300–600mg if ticagrelor/prasugrel contraindicated.
- **Anticoagulation** — LMWH: enoxaparin 1mg/kg SC BD (0.5mg/kg if eGFR <30 or age >75); or UFH infusion; fondaparinux is an alternative for NSTEMI. Continue until discharge or PCI.
- **Oxygen** only if SpO₂ <90% — routine high-flow oxygen in normoxic patients worsens outcomes (coronary vasoconstriction, increased infarct size)
- **GTN sublingual or spray** — for ongoing chest pain if systolic BP >90 mmHg. Absolutely avoid in RV infarction and if PDE5 inhibitors used within 24–48 hours.
- **Morphine IV** (2–4mg titrated) — for severe pain only; antiemetic (metoclopramide 10mg IV) co-administered. Note: opioids slow P2Y12 inhibitor absorption.
- **High-intensity statin** — atorvastatin 80mg as soon as possible
- **Beta-blocker** — reduces myocardial oxygen demand, improves outcomes. Avoid if cardiogenic shock, acute LV failure, significant bradycardia, or high-degree AV block.

### STEMI — Reperfusion Strategy

**Primary PCI** is the gold standard. Target: door-to-balloon time ≤90 minutes at a PCI-capable centre, or ≤120 minutes if transfer is required. For every 30-minute delay beyond this, mortality rises measurably. Drug-eluting stents preferred. Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT — aspirin + P2Y12) for at least 12 months post-stent.

**Thrombolysis** — used when primary PCI cannot be delivered within 120 minutes. Tenecteplase (weight-based IV bolus) or alteplase. The patient must still be transferred to a PCI centre after thrombolysis ("pharmaco-invasive" strategy). Failed thrombolysis (ST elevation not resolved by ≥50% at 60–90 min) → rescue PCI immediately.

**Absolute contraindications to thrombolysis:** Prior haemorrhagic stroke at any time; ischaemic stroke within 3 months; suspected aortic dissection; active internal bleeding (excluding menstruation); significant closed-head injury within 3 months; uncontrolled BP >180/110 despite treatment.

### NSTEMI — Risk Stratification and Angiography

| GRACE Score | Angiography Timing |
|------------|-------------------|
| Very high risk (instability: haemodynamic compromise, refractory ischaemia, life-threatening arrhythmia) | Immediate (<2 hours) |
| High risk (GRACE >140, troponin rise, dynamic ST changes) | Early (<24 hours) |
| Intermediate risk (GRACE 109–140) | Within 72 hours |
| Low risk (GRACE <109, no recurrent ischaemia) | Non-invasive testing or elective angiography |

### Secondary Prevention Post-ACS

| Medication | Duration / Target |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Aspirin 75mg OD | Indefinitely |
| Ticagrelor 90mg BD or clopidogrel 75mg OD | 12 months post-ACS (DAPT) |
| High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80mg) | Indefinitely; LDL target <55 mg/dL |
| ACEi (ramipril 2.5–10mg OD) or ARB | Indefinitely, especially if EF <40%, diabetes, or CKD |
| Beta-blocker (bisoprolol 2.5–10mg OD) | Indefinitely post-MI |
| Aldosterone antagonist (eplerenone 25–50mg) | If EF <40% with HF symptoms or diabetes |
| SGLT2 inhibitor | If EF <40% or HF post-MI (emerging evidence) |

### Lifestyle and Rehabilitation

- Strong advice and support to stop smoking
- Identify and treat diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and CKD
- Diet high in oily fish, fruit, vegetables, and fibre; low in saturated fats
- Daily exercise; refer to a formal cardiac rehabilitation programme
- Screen for depression and anxiety — both independently increase cardiovascular mortality

**Driving (UK):** Group 1 licence (car/motorcycle): may drive 1 week after successful PCI, or 4 weeks after ACS without successful PCI if EF >40%. Group 2 (HGV/PSV): must notify DVLA; may restart after 6 weeks if functional testing is satisfactory.

## Complications

Post-MI complications follow a predictable timeline — essential to recognise promptly:

| Complication | Timing | Key Features |
|-------------|--------|-------------|
| VF | Any time, especially first 24h | Most common cause of early death; defibrillate immediately |
| Cardiogenic shock | First 24h | Low output despite adequate filling; mortality ~40–50%; requires IABP, inotropes, or ECMO |
| Acute pericarditis | Within 1 week | Pleuritic chest pain, friction rub; treat with aspirin + colchicine; avoid NSAIDs (impair healing) |
| Papillary muscle rupture | 3–5 days | Acute severe MR, pulmonary oedema, new pansystolic murmur; surgical emergency |
| VSD (septal rupture) | 3–5 days | New holosystolic murmur at LLSB, biventricular failure; surgical emergency; 50% mortality in 1 week |
| Free wall rupture | 5 days–2 weeks | Haemopericardium, tamponade, PEA arrest; frequently fatal |
| RV infarction | Acute, with inferior STEMI | Hypotension + raised JVP + clear lungs; give IV fluids, avoid nitrates/diuretics |
| Dressler's syndrome | 2–10 weeks | Autoimmune pericarditis post-MI; fever, chest pain, raised ESR; treat with aspirin/NSAIDs; avoid anticoagulants |
| LV aneurysm | 4–6 weeks | Persistent ST elevation, risk of mural thrombus, recurrent VT; anticoagulate; consider excision |
| Late ventricular arrhythmias | 1–3 weeks | Consider 24h ECG monitoring before discharge after large MI |

## Clinical Insight

The inferior STEMI demands immediate right-sided leads (V4R) in every case. RV infarction is present in up to 40% of inferior STEMIs and completely changes management — volume resuscitation instead of vasodilators, and meticulously avoiding nitrates that can cause cardiovascular collapse.

Never administer thrombolytics without first excluding aortic dissection. A patient with tearing interscapular pain, BP asymmetry between arms, and ST changes who is thrombolysed will likely exsanguinate or develop haemopericardium. Take 10 minutes to get a CT first.

Troponin is not the diagnosis — it is a marker of myocardial injury from any cause. A troponin rise in a patient with sepsis, severe tachycardia, or respiratory failure is Type 2 MI from supply-demand mismatch. Rushing that patient to the catheterisation laboratory changes nothing and risks contrast nephropathy. Identify and treat the cause.

## Scenario Walkthrough

**The patient:** A 61-year-old man with hypertension and heavy smoking history. Central crushing chest pain radiating to the jaw, diaphoresis, and nausea for 90 minutes. ECG: 3mm ST elevation in II, III, and aVF with reciprocal ST depression in I and aVL. HR 88, BP 96/64.

**Step 1 — Diagnose and localise:** ST elevation in II, III, and aVF = **inferior STEMI**. The culprit vessel in ~90% of inferior STEMIs is the **right coronary artery (RCA)**. Reciprocal changes in I and aVL confirm this is a true STEMI, not pericarditis (which causes diffuse saddle-shaped elevation without reciprocal depression).

**Step 2 — The critical next step: right-sided leads immediately.** BP 96/64 in an inferior STEMI raises the immediate concern of **RV infarction** — present in up to 40% of inferior STEMIs when the occlusion is proximal. Do **V4R** before giving any vasodilator or diuretic. ST elevation ≥1mm in V4R confirms RV involvement.

**Step 3 — If RV infarction confirmed (ST elevation in V4R):** This patient is **preload-dependent**. Their hypotension is from RV failure, not LV failure.
- **Give IV fluid resuscitation** (0.9% saline boluses)
- **Absolutely avoid nitrates** — GTN will drop venous return, precipitate cardiovascular collapse
- **Avoid diuretics** — same mechanism
- Inotropes (dobutamine) if fluids alone do not restore BP

**Step 4 — Activate primary PCI:** Door-to-balloon ≤90 minutes at a PCI-capable centre. Give aspirin 300 mg + ticagrelor 180 mg loading doses immediately. Anticoagulate with LMWH or UFH. Oxygen only if SpO₂ <90%.

**Step 5 — Secondary prevention post-PCI:**
- DAPT (aspirin 75 mg + ticagrelor 90 mg BD) for 12 months
- Atorvastatin 80 mg, ramipril, bisoprolol
- Echo to assess EF — if EF <35% at 40 days despite optimal therapy → ICD referral

**Exam trigger:** *Inferior STEMI + hypotension* → before giving GTN, **get right-sided leads**. RV infarction is the answer. GTN in this context can kill the patient.
