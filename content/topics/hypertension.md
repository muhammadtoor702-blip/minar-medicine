## Pathophysiology

Blood pressure = Cardiac output × Total peripheral resistance. Hypertension results from a sustained increase in either or both.

**Primary (essential) hypertension** — 90–95% of cases. No single identifiable cause. Complex interplay of:
- Increased sympathetic nervous system activity
- RAAS overactivation → sodium retention
- Endothelial dysfunction → impaired vasodilation
- Genetic predisposition (polygenic)
- Environmental factors: excess sodium, obesity, alcohol, physical inactivity

**Secondary hypertension** — 5–10%. Always consider if: young patient, resistant hypertension, abrupt onset, unusual age of onset.

**End-organ damage from sustained HTN (Pathoma):** Hyaline arteriolosclerosis (benign HTN) → vessel wall thickening → ischaemia. Malignant HTN → fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles → rapidly progressive damage to kidneys (RPGN), eyes (grade 3–4 retinopathy), brain (encephalopathy).

## Clinical Reasoning

**HTN is almost always asymptomatic** until end-organ damage occurs. This is why screening is critical.

**The job when you see a raised BP:**
1. Confirm it is truly elevated (exclude white coat hypertension)
2. Stage the severity
3. Look for secondary causes
4. Assess for end-organ damage

**Hypertensive urgency vs emergency (Inner Circle):**
- **Urgency** — severely elevated BP (≥180/120) without acute end-organ damage. Oral medication, gradual reduction over 24–48h.
- **Emergency** — severely elevated BP + acute end-organ damage. IV treatment, controlled reduction.

**Hypertensive emergency target organs:**
- Brain: encephalopathy, intracranial haemorrhage (reduce BP by 25% in first hour)
- Heart: acute LV failure, ACS
- Aorta: dissection (target HR <60 AND systolic 100–120)
- Kidneys: acute oliguric renal failure (haematuria, proteinuria)
- Eyes: grade 3–4 retinopathy (flame haemorrhages, exudates, papilloedema)

**Staging (NICE):**
- Stage 1: Clinic BP ≥140/90 + ABPM/HBPM ≥135/85
- Stage 2: Clinic BP ≥160/100 + ABPM/HBPM ≥150/95
- Severe: Clinic systolic ≥180 or diastolic ≥120

## Workup

**Confirm with ABPM or HBPM** — never diagnose on a single clinic reading (white coat effect can elevate BP by 20+ mmHg).

**To assess end-organ damage:**
- **U&E** — renal function, electrolytes (hypokalaemia → Conn's)
- **Urinalysis** — dipstick protein + ACR (early renal damage)
- **ECG** — LVH (Sokolow-Lyon criteria, strain pattern)
- **Fundoscopy** — Keith-Wagener-Barker grading. Grade 3–4 = hypertensive emergency

**For secondary causes:**
- TFTs (hypothyroidism)
- Fasting glucose/HbA1c
- Plasma aldosterone:renin ratio (Conn's)
- 24h urinary catecholamines/metanephrines (phaeochromocytoma)
- Renal USS ± Doppler (renovascular disease, CKD)
- Low-dose dexamethasone suppression test (Cushing's)
- Sleep study (OSA)

## Management

### Lifestyle (all patients — mandatory, not optional)
- Salt restriction <6g/day
- DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)
- Weight loss — each 10kg lost reduces BP by ~5–10 mmHg
- Regular aerobic exercise ≥30 min, 5 days/week
- Alcohol reduction (<14 units/week)
- Smoking cessation (does not directly reduce BP but essential for cardiovascular risk)

### When to Start Medication
- Stage 1 HTN + cardiovascular risk >10% or diabetes or CKD or end-organ damage → treat
- Stage 2 HTN → always treat
- Severe (≥180/120) → treat same day, same visit

**Resistant HTN** = uncontrolled on 3 drugs including a diuretic. Investigate secondary causes. Consider spironolactone.

**Special situations:**
- Pregnancy: labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa. **Never ACEi/ARB** (teratogenic)
- CKD + proteinuria: ACEi/ARB first-line regardless of race
- HF with HFrEF: ACEi/ARB + beta-blocker (see HF topic)

**BP targets:**
- <140/90 for most (clinic) / <135/85 (ABPM)
- <130/80 for diabetes, CKD, cardiovascular disease
- <150/90 for age ≥80 (avoid over-treatment in frail elderly)

**IV drugs in hypertensive emergencies:**
- Aortic dissection: IV labetalol or esmolol (HR control first, then BP)
- Hypertensive encephalopathy: IV labetalol or sodium nitroprusside
- Eclampsia: IV labetalol or hydralazine
- Phaeochromocytoma crisis: IV phentolamine (alpha-blocker) FIRST — never beta-blocker alone (risks paradoxical hypertension)

## High Yield Pearls

- Always confirm with ABPM — white coat hypertension is common and over-treating it causes harm
- ACEi + ARB combination is absolutely contraindicated — doubles renal risk with no added benefit
- Hypokalaemia + HTN = Conn's syndrome until proven otherwise — check aldosterone:renin ratio
- Phaeochromocytoma: give alpha-blocker BEFORE beta-blocker — beta-blockade alone causes paradoxical HTN from unopposed alpha stimulation
- Renovascular HTN: worsening renal function after starting ACEi/ARB = bilateral renal artery stenosis or stenosis in a single functioning kidney
- ARBs provide the greatest reduction in LVH of all first-line antihypertensives (Inner Circle)
