## Overview

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for more than three months, with implications for health. It is classified by both GFR category (G1–G5) and albuminuria category (A1–A3), staged using the CGA system, and the combination determines the risk of progression and cardiovascular outcomes. CKD affects approximately 10% of the global population and is largely asymptomatic until advanced stages. The critical insight: patients with CKD are far more likely to die of cardiovascular disease than to reach end-stage renal disease. Managing the kidney and managing the heart are inseparable.

---

## Pathophysiology

Regardless of the initial insult, CKD progresses through a common final pathway: nephron loss triggers compensatory hyperfiltration in surviving nephrons. The increased glomerular pressure and flow initially preserves total GFR but over time accelerates glomerular injury, fibrosis, and further nephron loss — a self-perpetuating cycle. This is why addressing reversible contributors (hypertension, proteinuria, nephrotoxin exposure) early matters disproportionately; the slope of decline is modifiable even when the baseline cannot be recovered.

**Consequences of progressive nephron loss:**

| System | Mechanism | Consequence |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Fluid/electrolytes | Reduced Na/water excretion | Hypertension, oedema |
| Haematopoiesis | Reduced erythropoietin synthesis | Normocytic normochromic anaemia |
| Bone/calcium | Reduced 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D activation → hypocalcaemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism | Bone resorption, vascular calcification (CKD-MBD) |
| Acid-base | Reduced NH₄+ excretion and H+ secretion | Metabolic acidosis |
| Potassium | Reduced K+ excretion | Hyperkalaemia (worsened by ACEi/ARB) |
| Late uraemia (GFR <10–15) | Accumulation of nitrogenous waste | Encephalopathy, pericarditis, platelet dysfunction |

**Most common causes of CKD:**

| Cause | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Diabetic nephropathy | Leading cause of ESRD worldwide; Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodular lesion on biopsy |
| Hypertensive nephrosclerosis | Second most common in the developed world; diagnosis of exclusion |
| Glomerulonephritis | Biopsy required to define subtype and guide treatment |
| ADPKD | Most common inherited kidney disease; large kidneys with cysts bilaterally |
| Renovascular disease | Atherosclerotic (>60 years with vascular disease); fibromuscular dysplasia (young women) |
| Reflux nephropathy | Childhood vesicoureteric reflux → recurrent pyelonephritis → renal scarring |

## Clinical Presentation

CKD is characteristically **silent** until the GFR falls below 25–30 mL/min/1.73m². Symptoms when present are non-specific and frequently attributed to other diagnoses.

**KDIGO GFR categories:**

| Category | eGFR (mL/min/1.73m²) | Description |
|---------|----------------------|-------------|
| G1 | ≥90 | Normal/high (with kidney damage markers) |
| G2 | 60–89 | Mildly decreased |
| G3a | 45–59 | Mildly to moderately decreased |
| G3b | 30–44 | Moderately to severely decreased |
| G4 | 15–29 | Severely decreased |
| G5 | <15 | Kidney failure (ESRD) |

**KDIGO albuminuria categories (urine ACR):**

| Category | ACR (mg/mmol) | ACR (mg/g) | Description |
|---------|--------------|------------|-------------|
| A1 | <3 | <30 | Normal to mildly increased |
| A2 | 3–30 | 30–300 | Moderately increased (microalbuminuria) |
| A3 | >30 | >300 | Severely increased (macroalbuminuria) |

This patient is **G3b A3** — a high-risk combination with rapid progression likely. The KDIGO heat map shows this combination in red (very high risk of progression). The trajectory of eGFR (52 → 31 over two years = −10.5 mL/min per year) is more alarming than the absolute value.

**Uraemic features (G4–G5):** Fatigue, nausea, anorexia, cognitive slowing, peripheral neuropathy (restless legs syndrome), pruritus (from uraemic toxin deposition), uraemic pericarditis (friction rub — indication for urgent dialysis), platelet dysfunction (prolonged bleeding time, ecchymoses, epistaxis), renal osteodystrophy.

## Diagnosis

**Establish the stage and cause:**
- eGFR (CKD-EPI equation) + urine ACR — both abnormal for ≥3 months (two measurements separated by at least 90 days). Single readings may reflect acute illness.
- Renal ultrasound — all patients with CKD. Bilateral small kidneys = longstanding CKD; normal/large kidneys = DM, ADPKD, amyloid; asymmetric = renovascular disease.
- Urine microscopy — RBC casts or active sediment suggest active GN; urgent nephrology referral.
- Renal biopsy — if cause is unclear and histology would change management.

**Routine monitoring bloods:** U&E (including bicarbonate), eGFR, calcium, phosphate, PTH, 25-OH vitamin D, FBC, iron studies, LFTs, lipids, HbA1c, uric acid.

**Monitoring frequency by GFR category:**
- G1–G2: annually (if stable)
- G3a/3b: every 6–12 months
- G4: every 3–6 months
- G5: every 1–3 months

## Management

### Slowing Progression

**1. Blood pressure control**

Target <130/80 mmHg. **ACEi or ARB are first-line in any patient with proteinuria** — they reduce intraglomerular pressure by dilating the efferent arteriole, independently slowing CKD progression beyond their antihypertensive effect. A rise in creatinine of up to 30% within two weeks of starting ACEi/ARB is expected (afferent vasodilation reduces GFR transiently), acceptable, and does not warrant stopping the drug. Stop only if creatinine rises >30% or hyperkalaemia develops.

**2. SGLT2 inhibitors — a paradigm shift**

Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are now recommended for CKD in patients with **eGFR >25 and uACR >25 mg/mmol**, regardless of whether the patient has diabetes. The DAPA-CKD and EMPA-KIDNEY trials demonstrated significant reductions in progression to ESRD and cardiovascular events — effects that are largely independent of glucose lowering, working through reduced intraglomerular pressure (tubuloglomerular feedback), anti-inflammatory, and anti-fibrotic mechanisms. These are now first-line alongside ACEi/ARB.

**3. Finerenone**

A non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist. The FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD trials showed reduced CKD progression and cardiovascular events in diabetic nephropathy at CKD stages G3–G4 with residual albuminuria despite ACEi/ARB. Unlike spironolactone, it has greater selectivity and less gynaecomastia. Add in diabetic CKD with uACR >30 mg/mmol and eGFR >25 after optimising ACEi/ARB.

**4. Cardiovascular risk reduction**

The leading cause of death in CKD is cardiovascular disease, not renal failure. **Atorvastatin 20 mg** is recommended for all patients with CKD for CVD prevention regardless of baseline LDL — CKD is considered a high cardiovascular risk equivalent. Smoking cessation, aspirin in appropriate contexts, and aggressive BP control complete the cardiovascular strategy.

**5. Glycaemic control (diabetic CKD)**

Target HbA1c ~53 mmol/mol (7%), but individualise — insulin clearance falls as GFR falls, increasing hypoglycaemia risk. **Stop metformin when eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²** (risk of lactic acidosis). SGLT2 inhibitors provide renal protection at eGFR >25, even when glucose-lowering effect is diminished.

**6. Dietary modification**

Protein restriction 0.8 g/kg/day at G3–G4 reduces uraemic load and protein-derived acid load. Low potassium diet when eGFR <30. Low phosphate diet and phosphate binder education as GFR falls. Adequate caloric intake to prevent catabolism.

### Managing Complications

**Anaemia of CKD:**

Reduced erythropoietin production causes a normocytic, normochromic anaemia. Before attributing anaemia to CKD, exclude iron deficiency, B12/folate deficiency, and haemolysis — these are common and treatable.

- IV iron is preferred over oral in CKD — better absorbed (hepcidin upregulation reduces oral iron absorption) and better tolerated
- Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs — darbepoetin alfa, epoetin alfa) when Hb <10.0 g/dL after iron is repleted. Target Hb 10.0–12.0 g/dL. **Do not exceed 13.0 g/dL** — associated with increased cardiovascular events and death (TREAT trial: targeting higher Hb with darbepoetin alfa increased stroke risk)

**CKD-Mineral Bone Disorder (CKD-MBD):**

The cascade begins with reduced 1-alpha-hydroxylation of 25-OH vitamin D → reduced intestinal calcium absorption → low calcium → secondary hyperparathyroidism → PTH drives phosphate release from bone → hyperphosphataemia → vascular calcification. Phosphate binders and active vitamin D interrupt this cycle:
- **Sevelamer** (non-calcium-containing) — preferred; also reduces vascular calcification by binding gut phosphate
- **Calcium carbonate** — use cautiously in patients with vascular calcification (excess calcium load)
- **Alfacalcidol or calcitriol** — active vitamin D analogues (regular cholecalciferol requires 1-alpha-hydroxylation, which the failing kidney cannot perform)
- **Cinacalcet** — calcimimetic for secondary hyperparathyroidism not controlled by above; sensitises the parathyroid calcium-sensing receptor

**Metabolic acidosis:**

Oral sodium bicarbonate supplementation when serum bicarbonate <22 mEq/L. Correcting acidosis slows muscle protein catabolism, reduces bone resorption, and may independently slow CKD progression (metabolic acidosis activates the complement system, amplifying renal tubular injury).

**Hyperkalaemia:**

Dietary potassium restriction. Stop nephrotoxins. **Patiromer** or **sodium zirconium cyclosilicate (SZC/Lokelma)** for persistent hyperkalaemia — these novel potassium binders allow continuation of ACEi/ARB and SGLT2 inhibitors even in patients prone to hyperkalaemia, avoiding the trap of discontinuing nephroprotective therapy to manage a side effect.

### Preparation for Renal Replacement Therapy

Refer to nephrology when eGFR <30 — this allows time for education, modality counselling, and vascular access creation.

**RRT options:**

| Modality | Key Points |
|---------|-----------|
| Haemodialysis | 3× weekly, 4h sessions; AV fistula (gold standard access — mature 3–6 months after creation); tunnelled central venous catheter if access not ready |
| Peritoneal dialysis | Daily, home-based; CAPD (4 exchanges/day) or automated overnight (APD); preserves residual renal function longer than HD; contraindicated with abdominal adhesions |
| Kidney transplantation | Best outcomes and quality of life; **pre-emptive transplant** (before dialysis starts) is ideal — avoids dialysis-related complications and preserves residual function; living donor superior to deceased donor |

**Conservative management** is a valid, patient-centred option — particularly for elderly patients with significant comorbidities where dialysis offers survival benefit but substantially impairs quality of life. Conservative care focuses on symptom management, fluid balance, and advance care planning.

## Complications

- **Cardiovascular disease** — leading cause of death; risk is disproportionate even at moderate CKD stages (eGFR 45 confers similar CVD risk to established diabetes)
- **Anaemia** — fatigue, reduced quality of life, left ventricular hypertrophy
- **CKD-MBD** — fractures, bone pain, vascular calcification accelerating atherosclerosis
- **Peripheral neuropathy, restless legs syndrome, encephalopathy** — uraemic toxin accumulation
- **Uraemic pericarditis** — indication for urgent dialysis; anticoagulation risks tamponade
- **Increased infection risk** — impaired cellular and humoral immunity from uraemia
- **Medication toxicity** — accumulation of renally excreted drugs (metformin, NSAIDs, opioids, antibiotics)

---

## Clinical Insight

The trajectory of eGFR matters as much as the absolute value. An eGFR of 31 that has been stable for three years is fundamentally different from an eGFR of 31 that was 52 eighteen months ago. Always plot the slope. Extrapolating the decline predicts when ESRD will occur, when to refer for transplant assessment, and when to create vascular access — anticipating the endpoint avoids the disaster of a patient starting dialysis through a neck line because their fistula was created too late.

SGLT2 inhibitors protect the kidney through a mechanism largely independent of glucose lowering — reduced intraglomerular pressure from tubuloglomerular feedback (afferent arteriolar constriction), anti-inflammatory effects, and reduced renal oxygen consumption. They work in non-diabetic CKD and should be considered in all eligible patients (eGFR >25 + uACR >25) regardless of glycaemic status. The instinct to defer these drugs until "things are settled" misses the window of maximum benefit — early nephroprotection produces the largest absolute reduction in progression risk.

Atorvastatin 20 mg is specifically recommended in CKD rather than higher-potency statins or fibrates — it has the best evidence base for CVD reduction in CKD and avoids the statin myopathy risk that is amplified by urinary statin excretion impairment. Do not let the complexity of CKD management cause cardiovascular prevention to be deprioritised; it is the intervention most likely to prevent death.
