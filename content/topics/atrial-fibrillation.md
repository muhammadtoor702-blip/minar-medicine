## Pathophysiology

Atrial fibrillation (AF) results from chaotic, disorganised electrical activity in the atria — predominantly triggered by ectopic foci from the **pulmonary vein ostia**. Instead of organised P-wave driven contraction, the atria fire at 350–600 impulses per minute. The AV node acts as a gatekeeper, allowing only some impulses through → **irregularly irregular ventricular response**.

**Three key consequences:**
1. **Loss of atrial kick** — atria contribute 20–30% of ventricular filling. Particularly important in HFpEF and mitral stenosis where diastolic filling is already impaired.
2. **Stasis in left atrial appendage** → thrombus formation → cardioembolic stroke. AF increases stroke risk **5-fold**.
3. **Tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy** — uncontrolled rate over weeks/months → reversible dilated cardiomyopathy.

**Conditions associated with AF (Inner Circle — most common first):**
- Hypertensive heart disease (most common)
- Coronary artery disease
- Heart failure
- Valvular heart disease (especially mitral stenosis, mitral regurgitation)
- Hyperthyroidism — **always check TFTs in new AF**
- PE, COPD, OSA
- Alcohol ("holiday heart")
- Post-cardiac surgery
- Congenital heart disease (ASD)

**Classification (White Coat Companion):**
- **First detected** — first ever episode
- **Paroxysmal** — self-terminating, usually <48 hours
- **Persistent** — >7 days, requires cardioversion
- **Long-standing persistent** — >1 year
- **Permanent** — rate control accepted, no rhythm control

## Clinical Reasoning

**Irregularly irregular pulse = AF until proven otherwise** — always get an ECG.

**ECG findings:**
- **No P waves** (or fibrillatory baseline — irregular undulating baseline)
- **Irregularly irregular RR intervals** — the hallmark
- Narrow QRS (unless aberrant conduction or pre-existing BBB)

**Differential for irregular pulse:**
- Frequent ectopics (SVEs/PVEs) — ECG shows P waves with occasional premature beats
- Atrial flutter with variable block — sawtooth flutter waves at ~300 bpm, regular or regularly irregular
- Multifocal atrial tachycardia (MAT) — 3+ P wave morphologies, associated with COPD

**Is the patient haemodynamically stable?**
- Unstable (hypotension, pulmonary oedema, chest pain, reduced consciousness) → **immediate DC cardioversion**
- Stable → rate control vs rhythm control decision

## Workup

**Immediate:**
- 12-lead ECG — confirm diagnosis
- U&E, FBC, TFTs (thyrotoxicosis!), LFTs, glucose, coagulation
- Troponin if ischaemia suspected
- CXR — cardiomegaly, pulmonary oedema, infection

**Echocardiogram:**
- Assess LA size (enlarged LA = higher recurrence after cardioversion)
- LV function, valvular disease

**Transoesophageal echo (TOE):**
- If cardioversion planned and AF duration >48 hours
- Excludes left atrial thrombus before cardioversion

**Valvular AF** (mechanical valve, moderate-severe mitral stenosis, rheumatic heart disease) → **Warfarin only** regardless of CHA₂DS₂-VASc score (DOACs not validated).

## Management

### Haemodynamically Unstable → Immediate DC Cardioversion

### Haemodynamically Stable — Two Decisions

**Decision 1: Rate vs Rhythm Control**

| Strategy | Goal | Agents |
|---------|------|--------|
| **Rate control** | Ventricular rate <110 bpm at rest | Beta-blocker (bisoprolol) 1st line. Rate-limiting CCB (diltiazem/verapamil) if beta-blocker contraindicated. Digoxin if HF or sedentary. |
| **Rhythm control** | Restore and maintain sinus rhythm | Flecainide/propafenone (no structural heart disease). Amiodarone (structural heart disease/HF). DC cardioversion. Catheter ablation. |

**EAST-AFNET 4 trial (2020):** Early rhythm control reduces cardiovascular events compared to rate control alone — consider rhythm control in most patients, especially those with symptoms.

**Decision 2: Anticoagulation**
- DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban) preferred over warfarin in non-valvular AF
- Warfarin (INR 2–3) for valvular AF
- If AF >48 hours and cardioversion planned: **anticoagulate for ≥3 weeks before AND ≥4 weeks after** (or TOE to exclude thrombus first)
- Never cardiovert AF of unknown duration without anticoagulation or TOE

**Acute rate control agents:**
- IV metoprolol or diltiazem (avoid verapamil/diltiazem in HF — negative inotrope)
- IV digoxin — if acute HF (slow onset, poor rate control with exercise)
- IV amiodarone — if HF and haemodynamically borderline

## High Yield Pearls

- **Always check TFTs in new AF** — hyperthyroidism is a common and reversible precipitant; initial treatment is beta-blocker, not antithyroid drugs
- **Irregularly irregular pulse** — get the ECG, don't guess
- **AF >48 hours + cardioversion** — never cardiovert without ≥3 weeks anticoagulation or TOE to exclude thrombus
- **Valvular AF** — warfarin only (DOACs not validated in mechanical valves or rheumatic MS)
- **Rate target** — <110 bpm is adequate; strict control (<80 bpm) not shown to improve outcomes (RACE-II trial)
- **AF in hyperthyroidism** — beta-blocker for rate + treat thyroid; rhythm usually restores when euthyroid
