## Pathophysiology

Infective endocarditis (IE) is infection of the endocardial surface — almost always a native or prosthetic heart valve.

**The sequence (Pathoma):**
1. Turbulent blood flow or damaged endothelium → fibrin-platelet thrombus formation (NBTE — non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis)
2. Transient bacteraemia (dental procedure, skin breach, IVDU) → bacteria seed the sterile thrombus
3. Vegetation forms: bacteria + fibrin + platelets → protected from immune system and antibiotics

**Virulence determines the valve affected:**
- **Low virulence** (*Streptococcus viridans*, *Enterococcus*) → needs pre-existing valvular damage (mitral valve prolapse, rheumatic disease, bicuspid AoV) → **subacute endocarditis** (slow, indolent, weeks-months)
- **High virulence** (*S. aureus*) → infects **normal valves**. More common. Aggressive, acute course.

**IVDU endocarditis (Pathoma + Inner Circle):**
- *S. aureus* most common organism (injected skin flora)
- Preferentially infects **tricuspid valve** (right-sided) — injected material hits right heart first
- Right-sided IE → **septic pulmonary emboli** → multiple bilateral nodular opacities ± cavitation on CXR
- No Janeway lesions or Osler's nodes in right-sided IE (skin lesions from immune complex deposition — right-sided emboli go to lungs, not systemic circulation)

**Organisms by setting:**
| Setting | Organism |
|--------|---------|
| Community / dental | *S. viridans* (oral flora) |
| IVDU | *S. aureus* (most common) |
| Prosthetic valve <60 days | *S. epidermidis*, *S. aureus* |
| Prosthetic valve >60 days | *S. viridans*, *S. epidermidis* |
| Colon cancer / GI | *Streptococcus bovis* (S. gallolyticus) |
| Poor dental hygiene | HACEK organisms (*Haemophilus, Aggregatibacter, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, Kingella*) |

## Clinical Reasoning

**FROM JANE — classic peripheral stigmata:**
- **F**ever (most consistent finding)
- **R**oth spots — oval retinal haemorrhages with pale centre (immune complex)
- **O**sler's nodes — painful, tender nodules on finger/toe pulps (immune complex)
- **M**urmur — new or changed (90%, but may be absent in tricuspid IE)
- **J**aneway lesions — non-tender, haemorrhagic macules on palms and soles (embolic, septic)
- **A**naemia — chronic disease
- **N**ail changes — splinter haemorrhages (dark linear nail subungual streaks)
- **E**mboli — stroke, renal infarction, splenic infarction, septic pulmonary emboli (IVDU)

**Osler's vs Janeway:**
- Osler's = **painful** = immune complex = fingers
- Janeway = **painless** = septic emboli = palms/soles

**Duke Criteria for Diagnosis (Inner Circle):**

**Major criteria:**
- 2+ positive blood cultures with typical organism
- Echo showing vegetation, abscess, or dehiscence of prosthetic valve

**Minor criteria:**
- Predisposing cardiac lesion or IVDU
- Fever >38°C
- Embolic phenomena (Janeway lesions, septic pulmonary emboli, arterial emboli)
- Immunological phenomena (Osler's nodes, Roth spots, RF+, GN)
- Positive blood culture not meeting major criteria

**Diagnosis: 2 major, OR 1 major + 3 minor, OR 5 minor**

**Perivalvular abscess (Inner Circle):** Suspect if patient develops new conduction abnormality (AV block, bundle branch block) during IE treatment. Abscess extends into adjacent cardiac tissue → conduction system disruption. Most common with aortic valve (adjacent to AV node).

## Workup

**Blood cultures — cornerstone:**
- 3 sets from **different venipuncture sites**, ideally spaced 1 hour apart (to demonstrate persistent bacteraemia)
- Take **BEFORE** antibiotics — antimicrobials may sterilise cultures before diagnosis is confirmed
- 90% of IE is diagnosed by blood cultures alone

**Echo:**
- **TTE** first — sensitivity ~70% for native valve vegetations
- **TOE** — higher sensitivity (~95%), mandatory if: TTE negative but high suspicion, prosthetic valve, poor TTE windows, aortic root abscess suspected, planned surgery

**ECG:** Check for conduction abnormalities (PR prolongation → perivalvular abscess)

**Bloods:** FBC (anaemia, leukocytosis), CRP, ESR, U&E (renal involvement), urinalysis (haematuria from immune complex GN)

## Management

### Antibiotics — Principles

**Culture first, antibiotics second (Inner Circle)** — unless haemodynamically unstable.

**Empirical (before culture results):**
- Native valve: IV amoxicillin + gentamicin + flucloxacillin (covers streptococci + staph + enterococci)
- Prosthetic valve: IV vancomycin + gentamicin + rifampicin
- IVDU / MRSA risk: IV vancomycin

**Targeted (once cultures return):**
- *S. viridans* (penicillin-sensitive): IV penicillin G or ceftriaxone × 4 weeks. Ceftriaxone preferred (once daily dosing)
- *S. aureus* (MSSA): IV flucloxacillin × 4–6 weeks (6 weeks for prosthetic)
- *S. aureus* (MRSA): IV vancomycin × 4–6 weeks
- Enterococcus: ampicillin + gentamicin × 4–6 weeks

**Note (Inner Circle):** Oral antibiotics are **not** recommended for initial IE treatment.

### Indications for Surgery (urgent/emergency)

- **Heart failure** from valvular dysfunction — most common surgical indication
- **Uncontrolled infection** — abscess, fistula, persistent bacteraemia despite adequate antibiotics
- **Recurrent embolism** despite antibiotics
- **Large mobile vegetation** (>10mm) with high embolic risk
- Prosthetic valve IE (especially early)

### Prophylaxis

**Who needs prophylaxis?**
- Prior IE (highest risk)
- Prosthetic heart valves
- Unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease
- Repaired CHD with residual defects near prosthetic material

**When:** Dental procedures involving gum manipulation, dental extractions, periapical dental procedures.

**NOT indicated** for: routine dental cleaning, respiratory tract procedures, GI/GU procedures (Inner Circle).

**Drug:** Amoxicillin 2g oral 30–60 min before procedure. If penicillin allergic: clindamycin 600mg.

## High Yield Pearls

- Blood cultures BEFORE antibiotics — 3 sets from 3 different sites. This is the most important step.
- Osler's = **painful** (immune complex). Janeway = **painless** (septic emboli). Easy to mix up.
- IVDU + right-sided IE + bilateral pulmonary nodules on CXR = *S. aureus* → tricuspid valve → septic pulmonary emboli
- New AV block or PR prolongation in IE = perivalvular abscess until proven otherwise → urgent TOE + surgery
- *S. bovis* endocarditis → colonoscopy to exclude colorectal cancer (colonic bacteria don't normally enter blood)
- HACEK organisms: subacute endocarditis, poor dental hygiene, gram-negative rods — treat with ceftriaxone
