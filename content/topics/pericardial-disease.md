## Pathophysiology

The pericardium has two layers (visceral and parietal) with 15–50mL of fluid between them. Inflammation → exudate production → friction rub. If enough fluid accumulates → pericardial effusion → if rapid → cardiac tamponade.

**Causes:**
- **Viral (most common ~85%)** — Coxsackievirus B, Echovirus, EBV, CMV. Often post-viral immune-mediated.
- **Bacterial** — TB (most common bacterial cause worldwide), Staph, Strep. Purulent pericarditis = rare but life-threatening.
- **Autoimmune** — SLE, RA (serositis)
- **Post-cardiac injury** — Dressler's syndrome (weeks after MI or cardiac surgery)
- **Uraemia** — end-stage renal disease. Does NOT respond to NSAIDs. Treat with dialysis.
- **Malignancy** — breast, lung, lymphoma
- **Drugs** — hydralazine, procainamide

## Acute Pericarditis

**Diagnostic criteria — ≥2 of 4 (White Coat Companion):**
1. **Chest pain** — sharp, pleuritic, positional (worse supine, better leaning forward)
2. **Pericardial friction rub** — three-component, scratching, best heard at LLSB leaning forward
3. **ECG changes** — diffuse concave-up ("saddle-shaped") ST elevation + PR depression
4. **Pericardial effusion** — on echocardiogram

**ECG stages (Spodick's sign):**
- Stage 1: Diffuse saddle-shaped ST elevation + PR depression (hallmark)
- Stage 2: ST normalises, T waves flatten
- Stage 3: T wave inversions
- Stage 4: ECG normalises

**Distinguish from ACS:** Pericarditis ST elevation is diffuse (all territories), concave-up ("saddle-shaped"), with PR depression. ACS ST elevation is convex-up, focal (territory of one vessel), with reciprocal changes. No PR depression in ACS.

**Management:**
- **NSAIDs + Colchicine** — first-line. Ibuprofen 600mg TDS for 1–2 weeks.
- **Colchicine 0.5mg BD for 3 months** — halves recurrence rate (COPE, ICAP trials). Always add.
- Restrict strenuous activity until symptom-free + CRP normalised
- **Uraemic pericarditis** — dialysis (NSAIDs ineffective)
- **Post-MI pericarditis** — aspirin + colchicine (avoid NSAIDs — may impair myocardial healing)
- Steroids only if NSAIDs contraindicated or refractory — increases recurrence risk

**Recurrent pericarditis:**
- Long-term colchicine
- Anakinra (IL-1 receptor antagonist) — AIRTRIP trial — for colchicine-resistant recurrent pericarditis

## Pericardial Effusion

Fluid accumulation in the pericardial sac. May be transudative (HF, nephrotic, cirrhosis) or exudative (infection, malignancy, autoimmune).

**Clinical:**
- Often asymptomatic if small and slowly developing
- Muffled heart sounds, soft PMI, dullness at left lung base
- CXR — "water bottle" cardiomegaly (if large)
- ECG — low voltage, electrical alternans (alternating QRS axis)

**Management:** Treat underlying cause. Echo monitoring. Pericardiocentesis if large/symptomatic/unknown aetiology.

## Cardiac Tamponade

**Definition:** Pericardial effusion under pressure → impaired diastolic filling → reduced cardiac output. Results from either a large effusion OR rapid accumulation of small effusion.

**Beck's Triad:**
1. **Hypotension**
2. **JVD (elevated JVP)**
3. **Muffled heart sounds**

**Additional features:**
- **Pulsus paradoxus** — >10 mmHg fall in systolic BP during inspiration (pathognomonic). Normally there is a <10 mmHg drop. In tamponade: inspired RV expansion compresses LV further → marked fall in LV output.
- Tachycardia, narrowed pulse pressure
- **Equalization of cardiac pressures** on right heart catheterisation
- ECG: low voltage + electrical alternans
- Echo: diastolic collapse of RA and RV (highly sensitive)

**Management:**
- IV fluids — maintain preload (tamponade is preload-dependent)
- **Urgent pericardiocentesis** — non-haemorrhagic tamponade
- Surgical drainage — haemorrhagic (traumatic) tamponade
- Do NOT give diuretics or vasodilators (reduce preload → haemodynamic collapse)

## Constrictive Pericarditis

Fibrous scarring of pericardium → rigid, non-compliant pericardium → restricted diastolic filling.

**Causes:** TB (most common worldwide), connective tissue disease, prior pericarditis (viral/post-cardiac surgery), radiation therapy.

**Clinical:**
- Progressive right heart failure (elevated JVP, peripheral oedema, hepatomegaly, ascites)
- **Kussmaul's sign** — paradoxical rise in JVP with inspiration (normally JVP falls with inspiration)
- **Pericardial knock** — early diastolic sound (abrupt halt of cardiac filling by rigid pericardium)
- No pulsus paradoxus (unlike tamponade)

**Diagnosis:** CXR (pericardial calcification), cardiac MRI or CT (pericardial thickening >4mm), right heart catheterisation (equalised diastolic pressures, dip-plateau waveform)

**Management:** Pericardiectomy (definitive)

## High Yield Pearls

- Pericarditis: diffuse saddle-shaped ST elevation + PR depression — this ECG pattern with pleuritic pain = pericarditis
- Always add colchicine to NSAIDs — halves recurrence rate
- Uraemic pericarditis → dialysis (not NSAIDs)
- Tamponade: Beck's triad + pulsus paradoxus + electrical alternans → urgent pericardiocentesis
- Constrictive pericarditis vs tamponade: both have elevated JVP, both have equalised pressures. Kussmaul's sign = constrictive (JVP rises with inspiration, not falls). Pulsus paradoxus = tamponade.
- Do NOT give diuretics in tamponade — patient is preload-dependent
