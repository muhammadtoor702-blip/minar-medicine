## Pathophysiology

Stable CAD (chronic coronary syndrome) results from fixed atherosclerotic narrowing of one or more coronary arteries. Unlike ACS (plaque rupture with thrombosis), stable CAD involves a **stable plaque** that limits blood flow during increased demand but not at rest.

**The ischaemia threshold:** At rest, even severe stenosis (>70–80%) may permit adequate flow. During exercise or stress, O₂ demand rises → fixed stenosis cannot increase flow to match → subendocardial ischaemia → angina.

**Angina variants:**

| Type | Mechanism | Key features |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Stable angina** | Fixed stenosis >70%, exertional demand exceeds supply | Predictable, on exertion, relieved by rest or GTN in <5 min |
| **Vasospastic (Prinzmetal's)** | Coronary artery spasm, often on normal arteries | At rest, often nocturnal/early morning. Transient ST elevation during episode. Young patients, smokers. |
| **Microvascular angina (Cardiac Syndrome X)** | Endothelial dysfunction of small vessels | Angina + positive stress test + normal coronary angiogram. More common in women. |

**Risk factors for CAD:**
- Modifiable: hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, smoking, obesity, physical inactivity
- Non-modifiable: age (men >45, women >55), male sex, family history (first-degree relative, men <55, women <65)
- **Diabetes = CAD risk equivalent** (Inner Circle)

## Clinical Reasoning

**Typical angina = all three:**
1. Central chest discomfort (pressure, tightness, heaviness — not sharp)
2. Precipitated by exertion or emotional stress
3. Relieved by rest or GTN within 5 minutes

**Atypical angina = 2 of 3** (especially in women, elderly, diabetics)

**Non-cardiac chest pain = 0–1 of 3**

**Pre-test probability (ESC 2019):** Determines investigation strategy.
- Low (<15%): no further cardiac testing needed — look for other causes
- Intermediate (15–85%): functional stress testing or CT coronary angiography (CTCA)
- High (>85%): treat as CAD, consider direct invasive angiography

**Differential for exertional chest pain:**
- Stable angina — typical features, risk factors
- GORD — food-related, burning, regurgitation, improves with antacids
- Musculoskeletal — reproduced by palpation, specific movement
- Anaemia — exertional dyspnoea/angina, pallor, low Hb
- Aortic stenosis — exertional symptoms + ejection systolic murmur radiating to carotids

## Workup

**Resting ECG:** May show LVH, prior Q waves (old MI), LBBB, ST-T changes. Often **normal** in stable CAD.

**High-risk features on exercise test:**
- ST depression >2mm at low workload (<5 METs)
- ST depression in ≥5 leads
- Failure of SBP to rise with exercise (flat or falling)
- Sustained VT
- Duke Treadmill Score ≤−11 = high risk → proceed directly to invasive coronary angiography

**Nuclear perfusion imaging (SPECT):**
- Reversible defect (ischaemia on stress, normal at rest) = inducible ischaemia, viable myocardium
- Fixed defect (abnormal at both stress and rest) = scar tissue, old infarct
- Normal = excellent prognosis (annual risk <1%)

**Invasive coronary angiography:** Gold standard. Required before revascularisation. Defines anatomy, severity, suitability for PCI vs CABG.

**Bloods:** Fasting lipids (LDL target), HbA1c, renal function (before contrast), thyroid (exclude secondary dyslipidaemia)

## Management

### 1. Secondary Prevention (all patients)

- **Antiplatelet:** Aspirin 75mg daily indefinitely. Clopidogrel if aspirin intolerant.
- **Statin:** High-intensity (atorvastatin 80mg or rosuvastatin 20–40mg). Target LDL <1.4 mmol/L (very high CV risk — ESC 2019).
- **ACE inhibitor/ARB:** If LV dysfunction, diabetes, or CKD. Consider in all stable CAD.
- **Beta-blocker:** If prior MI or LV dysfunction.
- **BP control:** Target <130/80.
- **Glycaemic control:** HbA1c <53 mmol/mol.
- **Lifestyle:** Smoking cessation, cardiac rehabilitation, Mediterranean diet, exercise.

### 2. Anti-anginal Therapy (symptom control)

**First-line — Beta-blocker:**
- Reduces HR → increases diastolic filling → reduces myocardial O₂ demand
- Also prolongs survival post-MI

**Second-line / alternative — Calcium channel blocker:**
- Rate-limiting (diltiazem, verapamil): negative chronotropic, negative inotropic
- Dihydropyridine (amlodipine, nifedipine): vasodilatory, no HR effect
- **Vasospastic angina:** CCB first-line. Beta-blockers contraindicated (can worsen spasm — Inner Circle).

**Nitrates:**
- Short-acting GTN (sublingual/spray) — for acute episodes
- Long-acting nitrates (ISMN) — prophylaxis
- Require nitrate-free interval (8 hours) to prevent tolerance

**Third-line:**
- Ivabradine — reduces HR without BP or contractility effect (if CCB/BB contraindicated)
- Ranolazine — reduces late Na+ current, reduces ischaemia
- Nicorandil — K+ channel opener + nitrate effect

### 3. Revascularisation

**PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention):**
- Preferred for single/two-vessel disease
- Drug-eluting stents reduce restenosis
- DAPT post-stent: aspirin + clopidogrel for 6–12 months

**CABG (coronary artery bypass grafting):**
- Preferred for: left main stenosis, three-vessel CAD especially with diabetes, reduced LVEF, complex disease (high SYNTAX score)
- Survival benefit over PCI in multivessel disease + diabetes (FREEDOM trial)
- LIMA (left internal mammary artery) → LAD graft: gold standard (>90% patency at 10 years)

**ISCHEMIA trial (2020):** In stable CAD with moderate-severe ischaemia, routine early invasive strategy did NOT reduce MI or death compared to optimal medical therapy — supports aggressive medical management first.

## High Yield Pearls

- GTN relieves typical angina within 5 minutes — if not, consider ACS
- Vasospastic (Prinzmetal's) angina: beta-blockers worsen spasm — use calcium channel blockers
- Hold beta-blockers/CCBs/nitrates 48h before exercise stress test (unless testing anti-anginal efficacy)
- Falling BP with exercise = high-risk feature on stress test → refer for angiography
- Three-vessel CAD + diabetes → CABG superior to PCI (FREEDOM trial)
- ISCHEMIA trial: optimal medical therapy is as good as routine PCI for stable CAD — don't rush to the cath lab
