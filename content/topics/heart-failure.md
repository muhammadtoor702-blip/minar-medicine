## Pathophysiology

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome in which the heart cannot pump sufficient blood to meet metabolic demands, or can only do so at the cost of elevated filling pressures. It is the final common pathway of many cardiac diseases.

**Compensatory mechanisms and why they fail (Pathoma):**

When cardiac output falls, the body activates compensatory mechanisms:
1. **RAAS activation** → angiotensin II → aldosterone → sodium and water retention → increased preload
2. **Sympathetic activation** → tachycardia + vasoconstriction → increased afterload
3. **Ventricular hypertrophy/remodelling** → initially compensatory, then maladaptive

These mechanisms initially maintain cardiac output but ultimately worsen HF — causing volume overload, ventricular remodelling, and progressive deterioration. This is the rationale for ACEi, beta-blockers, and spironolactone.

**Left vs Right heart failure:**
- **Left HF** → fluid backs up into pulmonary circulation → pulmonary oedema → dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND
- **Right HF** → fluid backs up into systemic circulation → peripheral oedema, raised JVP, hepatomegaly, ascites
- Most patients have **biventricular** failure eventually

**High output HF** (White Coat Companion) — elevated CO that cannot meet massively increased metabolic demands: severe anaemia, hyperthyroidism, AV fistula, beriberi (B1 deficiency), Paget's disease, pregnancy, sepsis.

**Cor pulmonale** — isolated RV failure secondary to pulmonary hypertension (from lung disease, not left heart disease). ECG shows RBBB, RVH, right axis deviation.

## Clinical Reasoning — The Differential

**Left HF symptoms:** Dyspnoea on exertion → orthopnoea → paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea → dyspnoea at rest

**Right HF symptoms:** Peripheral oedema, weight gain, abdominal fullness (hepatomegaly), nausea

**Classic examination findings:**
- S3 gallop (volume overload, dilated ventricle) — highly specific for HF
- S4 gallop (stiff, non-compliant ventricle) — HFpEF, hypertension, acute MI
- Displaced apex beat (LV dilation)
- Raised JVP
- Bibasal crepitations (pulmonary oedema)

**CXR findings (ABCDE of pulmonary oedema):**
- **A**lveolar oedema (bat-wing shadowing)
- **B** lines (Kerley B — horizontal lines at lung bases, 1–2cm, perpendicular to pleura)
- **C**ardiomegaly (cardiothoracic ratio >0.5 on PA film)
- **D**iversion of blood flow (upper lobe venous diversion)
- **E**ffusions (pleural — usually bilateral, right > left)

**BNP/NT-proBNP:**
- High sensitivity, high negative predictive value
- BNP >400 pg/mL strongly suggests HF
- Normal BNP in untreated patient effectively excludes HF

**Differential for acute breathlessness + oedema:**
- COPD/asthma exacerbation — wheeze, hyperinflation, no raised JVP
- PE — pleuritic pain, risk factors for DVT, no oedema
- Pneumonia — fever, productive cough, consolidation
- Nephrotic syndrome — oedema without raised JVP, heavy proteinuria
- Liver failure — hypoalbuminaemia + stigmata of liver disease

## Workup

**Bloods:** BNP/NT-proBNP (diagnosis + monitoring), FBC (anaemia worsens HF), U&E (baseline before ACEi/diuretics), LFTs, TFTs (hypothyroidism causes HF), HbA1c, lipids

**ECG:** LVH, AF, ischaemic changes, LBBB (indicates CRT benefit)

**CXR:** As above

**Echo (TTE) — essential:**
- Determines EF → guides treatment
- Identifies structural cause (valvular disease, wall motion abnormalities)
- Assesses diastolic function, pulmonary pressures
- Required before starting evidence-based therapy

**Right heart catheterisation:**
- Increased PCWP (left HF)
- Increased RA pressure, RVEDP (right HF)
- Used when diagnosis unclear or pre-transplant evaluation

## Management

### Acute Decompensated HF

- **Positioning** — sit upright (reduces preload)
- **O₂** — target SpO₂ >94%
- **IV furosemide** — 2.5× home oral dose given IV (IV has better bioavailability in acute setting). Monitor urine output, electrolytes, creatinine
- **GTN infusion** — if systolic BP >90 mmHg. Reduces preload and afterload
- **CPAP/BiPAP (NIPPY)** — for acute pulmonary oedema not responding to initial measures
- **Inotropes** (dobutamine, milrinone) — if cardiogenic shock, refractory to diuretics
- **Avoid** NSAIDs (worsen fluid retention), beta-blockers in acute decompensation

**Diuretics** (furosemide/torsemide) — symptomatic relief of congestion, not mortality benefit.

**Device therapy:**
- **ICD** — if EF <35% despite GDMT (sudden cardiac death prevention)
- **CRT (biventricular pacing)** — if EF <35% + LBBB + QRS >120ms (improves cardiac synchrony)
- **Heart transplant / LVAD** — end-stage refractory HF

### Chronic HFpEF (White Coat Companion)

No therapy proven to reduce mortality. Focus on:
- Treat underlying cause (hypertension, AF, DM, obesity)
- Diuretics for volume overload
- SGLT2 inhibitors show emerging benefit (EMPEROR-Preserved trial)

**NYHA classification:**

| Class | Symptoms | Management focus |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| I | No symptoms, normal activity | ACEi + beta-blocker |
| II | Symptoms with moderate exertion | Add spironolactone, SGLT2i |
| III | Symptoms with ADLs | Consider CRT/ICD if indicated |
| IV | Symptoms at rest | Advanced therapies (LVAD, transplant) |

## High Yield Pearls

- BNP has high negative predictive value — a normal BNP effectively excludes HF in an undiagnosed breathless patient
- Four pillars of HFrEF ALL reduce mortality — don't just give furosemide and send home
- Never start a beta-blocker in acute decompensated HF — stabilise first, then introduce
- SGLT2 inhibitors are now first-line in HFrEF regardless of diabetes status
- The most common precipitant of decompensation is medication non-compliance or dietary indiscretion (excess salt/fluid)
- ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan) is superior to ACEi alone — use it in patients tolerating ACEi who remain symptomatic
