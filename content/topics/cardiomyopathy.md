## Overview

Cardiomyopathy is primary disease of the heart muscle. Four main types: dilated, hypertrophic, restrictive, and arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC).

---

## Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)

**The most common form of cardiomyopathy.** Four-chamber dilation + systolic dysfunction (EF <40%).

**Causes (Pathoma):**
- **Idiopathic/genetic** — most common (~50%), autosomal dominant mutations (titin, lamin A/C)
- **Myocarditis** — viral (Coxsackievirus A and B most common), Chagas disease (*T. cruzi* — South America)
- **Toxic** — alcohol (most common toxic cause), cocaine, doxorubicin (anthracycline chemotherapy — dose-dependent), trastuzumab
- **Peripartum cardiomyopathy** — onset in last month of pregnancy or within 5 months of delivery. LVEF <45%. Treat as HFrEF. Risk in subsequent pregnancies.
- **Takotsubo (stress cardiomyopathy)** — triggered by severe emotional/physical stress (high catecholamines). Apical ballooning on TTE. Reversible. Mimics STEMI (anterior ST elevation, troponin rise, but culprit arteries clean).
- **Tachycardia-induced** — chronic uncontrolled tachyarrhythmia (AF)
- **Lyme disease** — Borrelia burgdorferi; AV block + carditis
- **Haemochromatosis** — iron deposition → restrictive/dilated phenotype

**Clinical:** Systolic heart failure. Mitral/tricuspid regurgitation (functional). Arrhythmias.

**Management:** HFrEF protocol (ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i). ICD if EF <35%. Heart transplant for end-stage.

## Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)

**The most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes.**

**Pathophysiology:** Genetic mutation in sarcomere proteins (most commonly β-myosin heavy chain or myosin-binding protein C — autosomal dominant) → disorganised, hypertrophied myocytes → **asymmetric septal hypertrophy** → dynamic left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction.

**LVOT obstruction mechanism:** Thickened septum + systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the mitral valve → narrows LVOT → dynamic obstruction. Dynamic = changes with loading conditions.

**Murmur behaviour (key for exams):**
- **Valsalva, standing** (↓ preload → smaller LV → worse obstruction) → murmur **louder**
- **Squatting, leg raise, handgrip** (↑ preload → larger LV → less obstruction) → murmur **softer**
- (Opposite of AS and MR — remember: HCM murmur is the weird one)

**Clinical features (White Coat Companion):**
- Exertional chest pain, dyspnoea, syncope (fixed cardiac output)
- **Sudden cardiac death** — from ventricular arrhythmia. Often first presentation. Most common in young athletes (intense exercise ↑ outflow gradient)
- Harsh systolic murmur at LLSB (outflow tract turbulence)
- S4 (non-compliant, stiff ventricle)
- Bifid carotid pulse (pulsus bisferiens — double-peaked from initial ejection then obstruction)

**Risk factors for SCD in HCM:** Prior cardiac arrest/sustained VT, family history of SCD, septal thickness ≥30mm, unexplained syncope, LVOT gradient >30 mmHg, non-sustained VT on Holter.

**Management:**
- **Beta-blocker** (first-line) — reduces HR, increases diastolic filling time, reduces outflow gradient
- **Verapamil** (rate-limiting CCB) — if beta-blocker not tolerated
- **Avoid**: vasodilators (reduce preload/afterload → worsen obstruction), digoxin, diuretics (cautiously)
- **ICD** — for high-risk SCD prevention (replace, not add to, medical therapy for SCD)
- **Myectomy or alcohol septal ablation** — for refractory severe HVOT obstruction
- **Disopyramide** — negative inotrope, reduces dynamic obstruction

## Restrictive Cardiomyopathy

**Pathophysiology:** Infiltration of myocardium → impaired diastolic relaxation (stiff ventricle) → elevated filling pressures → diastolic HF. Systolic function often preserved.

**Causes (RASHE mnemonic):**
- **R**adiation
- **A**myloidosis (most common) — AL amyloid (myeloma) or ATTR (hereditary or senile)
- **S**arcoidosis
- **H**aemochromatosis
- **E**ndomyocardial fibrosis (Loeffler's — eosinophilic) / Fabry disease

**Amyloidosis (important):**
- AL: low voltage ECG + echo "sparkling" myocardium + thick walls
- ATTR: increasingly recognised; treated with tafamidis (ATTR-CM trial)
- "Apple green birefringence" on Congo red stain (Pathoma)

**Clinical:** Prominent right heart failure, dyspnoea, fatigue. Biatrial enlargement.

**Management:** Treat underlying cause. Diuretics for congestion. Heart transplant for refractory cases.

---

## Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)

**Genetic disorder** — desmosomal protein mutations → fibrofatty replacement of RV myocardium → VT with LBBB morphology (ectopic from RV).

**Presents as:** Ventricular arrhythmias, palpitations, syncope, SCD in young adults. Prominent in athletes ("Padua sign" — epsilon wave on ECG, T-wave inversions V1–V3).

**Diagnosis:** Modified Task Force criteria (ECG, echo, MRI, biopsy, genetics).

**Management:** ICD for high-risk. Beta-blocker. Restriction from competitive sports.

## High Yield Pearls

- HCM murmur increases with Valsalva/standing (↓ preload), decreases with squatting (↑ preload) — opposite of AS/MR
- HCM is most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes — screening ECG in athletes is key
- Peripartum cardiomyopathy: onset last month of pregnancy → 5 months postpartum. Treat as HFrEF. Risk recurrence in future pregnancies.
- Takotsubo: stress-triggered, apical ballooning on echo, troponin rise, normal coronaries. Reversible.
- Doxorubicin (adriamycin) causes dose-dependent cardiomyopathy — monitor echo before and during chemotherapy
- Amyloidosis: low-voltage ECG + thick walls on echo = mismatch = red flag. "Sparkling" appearance.
