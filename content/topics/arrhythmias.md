## Pathophysiology

Arrhythmias arise from three mechanisms:
1. **Abnormal automaticity** — ectopic pacemaker fires spontaneously (e.g., junctional rhythm, accelerated idioventricular)
2. **Re-entry** — electrical impulse travels in a circuit (requires two pathways with different conduction speeds/refractory periods). Most common mechanism. Causes: AF, AFL, AVNRT, WPW, VT.
3. **Triggered activity** — early or delayed afterdepolarisations. Causes: torsades de pointes (early), digoxin toxicity (delayed).

**The conduction system:**
SA node (dominant pacemaker, 60–100 bpm) → AV node (gatekeeper, delays conduction) → Bundle of His → left/right bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.

## Approach to Tachyarrhythmias

**First question: haemodynamically stable or unstable?**

<div style="background:#fff0f0;border-left:3px solid #cc0000;padding:1rem 1.25rem;margin:1rem 0;border-radius:0 8px 8px 0">
<strong style="color:#8B0000">Haemodynamically unstable</strong> (hypotension, chest pain, acute HF, reduced consciousness) → <strong>immediate synchronised DC cardioversion</strong>, regardless of rhythm. Do not delay for drug treatment.
</div>

**Second question: narrow or wide complex?**
- **Narrow QRS (<120ms):** Supraventricular (above His bundle). SA node, atria, or AV junction.
- **Wide QRS (≥120ms):** Ventricular OR supraventricular with aberrant conduction (BBB, WPW). Treat as ventricular until proven otherwise.

## Narrow Complex Tachycardias

### Sinus Tachycardia
Rate 100–220 bpm. P waves present and upright in II. Normal P-QRS relationship. **Not a primary arrhythmia** — treat the cause (pain, fever, dehydration, PE, anaemia, hyperthyroidism).

### AVNRT (AV Node Re-entrant Tachycardia)
Most common paroxysmal SVT. Re-entry circuit within or adjacent to AV node (fast and slow pathways). Rate 150–250 bpm. **P waves hidden within or just after QRS** (retrograde P waves, pseudo-R' in V1, pseudo-S in inferior leads).

**Management (Inner Circle):**
1. **Vagal manoeuvres** — Valsalva (most effective), carotid sinus massage (avoid if carotid bruit), cold water immersion. Increase parasympathetic tone → slows AV conduction → breaks circuit.
2. **Adenosine IV** — 6mg rapid push, then 12mg if fails, then 18mg. Very short half-life (~10 seconds). Causes AV block → terminates re-entry. Warn patient of brief chest tightness/flushing. Contraindicated in asthma (causes bronchospasm).
3. **Verapamil or diltiazem** (if adenosine fails or contraindicated) — rate-limiting CCB.
4. **DC cardioversion** if drugs fail or haemodynamically unstable.
5. **Long-term:** Catheter ablation (slow pathway) — curative.

### Atrial Flutter
Macro re-entry circuit in right atrium. Rate ~300 bpm (atrial) with 2:1 or variable block → ventricular rate 150 bpm (2:1) or 75–100 bpm (4:1). **Sawtooth flutter waves** at 300 bpm best seen in inferior leads and V1. Regular or regularly irregular.

**Management:** Rate control (as for AF), anticoagulation (same stroke risk as AF), cardioversion, catheter ablation (cavotricuspid isthmus ablation — highly effective, >90% cure).

## Wide Complex Tachycardias

**Assume ventricular tachycardia (VT) until proven otherwise.** Features favouring VT over SVT with aberrancy:
- AV dissociation (P waves and QRS independent — pathognomonic of VT)
- **Fusion beats** (sinus + ventricular complex fused — pathognomonic)
- **Capture beats** (sinus impulse briefly captures ventricles — pathognomonic)
- Concordance in V1–V6 (all positive or all negative)
- Extreme axis deviation ("northwest axis")
- QRS >160ms (very wide)
- History of prior MI or structural heart disease → almost always VT

**Brugada criteria / Vereckei algorithm** — systematic tools to distinguish VT from SVT with BBB.

### Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)
≥3 consecutive ventricular beats at >100 bpm. Monomorphic (same QRS morphology) vs polymorphic.

**Management:**
- **Pulseless VT → CPR + defibrillation (unsynchronised)**
- **Haemodynamically unstable with pulse → synchronised cardioversion**
- **Haemodynamically stable:** IV amiodarone (150mg over 10 min, then infusion). Procainamide (US). Lidocaine (2nd line).
- **Long-term:** Treat underlying cause. ICD if recurrent/structural heart disease.

### Torsades de Pointes
Polymorphic VT with rotating QRS axis around the isoelectric line ("twisting of the points"). Associated with **prolonged QT interval**.

**Causes of long QT (Inner Circle):**
- **Drugs** — antiarrhythmics (amiodarone, sotalol, quinidine), antipsychotics (haloperidol, quetiapine), antibiotics (azithromycin, fluconazole), methadone
- **Electrolytes** — hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia (the three Hs)
- **Congenital** — Romano-Ward (autosomal dominant), Jervell-Lange-Nielsen (with deafness)

**Management:**
- **IV magnesium sulphate 2g** — first-line for stable torsades (Inner Circle)
- Cardioversion if pulseless/haemodynamically unstable
- Remove offending drug, correct electrolytes
- Temporary pacing (increase HR → shorten QT) for bradycardia-induced torsades
- Long-term: beta-blocker, ICD for congenital LQTS

### Ventricular Fibrillation
Chaotic, uncoordinated ventricular activity. No cardiac output. → **CPR + immediate defibrillation.**

## Bradyarrhythmias

### Sick Sinus Syndrome
SA node dysfunction → bradycardia, pauses, alternating tachy-brady (tachy-brady syndrome). Causes: ageing, cardiomyopathy, ischaemia. Treatment: permanent pacemaker.

### Heart Blocks

**First-degree:** Prolonged PR >200ms. Benign. No treatment.

**Second-degree Mobitz Type I (Wenckebach):** Progressive PR lengthening → dropped QRS. Block at AV node. Often benign (athletes, inferior MI — transient, resolves with atropine). Rarely needs pacing.

**Second-degree Mobitz Type II:** Fixed PR interval → intermittent dropped QRS without warning. Block below AV node (His-Purkinje). More serious — high risk of progression to complete block. **Pacemaker indicated.**

**Third-degree (complete) heart block:** P waves and QRS completely dissociated. Escape rhythm from junction (narrow, 40–60 bpm) or ventricle (wide, 20–40 bpm). Causes: inferior MI (transient, treat with atropine), anterior MI (permanent damage), Lyme disease, drugs (beta-blocker, CCB, digoxin overdose). **Permanent pacemaker.**

**Remember:** RCA → AV node blood supply → inferior MI → transient AV block. Anterior MI → bundle branches → permanent block.

## Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW)

Accessory pathway (bundle of Kent) connects atria to ventricles, bypassing AV node → **delta wave** (slurred QRS upstroke), **short PR** (<120ms), wide QRS.

**During AF in WPW:** Rapid conduction via accessory pathway (no rate limitation) → extremely fast ventricular rate → VF → SCD.

**Danger: NEVER use AV-nodal blocking drugs (adenosine, verapamil, digoxin) in AF with WPW** — blocks AV node, forces all conduction via accessory pathway → VF.

**Management of AF in WPW:** Procainamide or flecainide IV (slow accessory pathway conduction). Cardioversion if unstable. Long-term: **catheter ablation** of accessory pathway (curative).

## High Yield Pearls

- Haemodynamically unstable tachycardia → synchronised cardioversion immediately, regardless of type
- Wide complex tachycardia = VT until proven otherwise — don't give verapamil blindly
- Torsades → IV magnesium sulphate first-line. Check QT-prolonging drugs and electrolytes
- Adenosine: contraindicated in asthma (bronchospasm), WPW with AF (can precipitate VF)
- AV dissociation + fusion beats + capture beats = diagnostic of VT
- WPW + AF → NEVER adenosine/verapamil/digoxin → catheter ablation is curative
- Mobitz II → permanent pacemaker (high risk of sudden complete block)
