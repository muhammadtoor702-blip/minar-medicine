## Overview

Psychiatric emergencies in A&E encompass acute psychosis, severe self-harm or suicidal behaviour, extreme agitation, and medical presentations mimicking psychiatric illness. They require the same systematic, structured approach as any other acute emergency — airway, patient safety, and exclusion of organic causes first. The intersection of mental health law, capacity assessment, and acute medical care makes psychiatric emergencies uniquely complex. In England and Wales, the Mental Health Act 1983 (amended 2007) and the Mental Capacity Act 2005 provide the legislative framework for assessment and treatment without consent. A critical principle: **altered mental state is always organic until proven otherwise** — hypoglycaemia, hypoxia, intracranial pathology, drug toxicity, and metabolic derangement all cause behavioural disturbance and must be excluded before attributing symptoms to primary psychiatric illness.

## Pathophysiology

### Organic Causes of Acute Behavioural Disturbance

Any systematic approach to acute agitation or psychosis must first exclude:

| System | Causes |
|--------|--------|
| **Metabolic** | Hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia, hypercalcaemia, uraemia, hepatic encephalopathy, thyroid storm, Addisonian crisis |
| **Neurological** | Intracranial haemorrhage, stroke, CNS infection (encephalitis), postictal state, space-occupying lesion, non-convulsive status epilepticus |
| **Toxicological** | Stimulants (cocaine, amphetamines, MDMA), cannabis psychosis, alcohol withdrawal (delirium tremens), anticholinergic toxidrome, serotonin syndrome, NMS |
| **Hypoxia/Sepsis** | Delirium from any cause — always check SpO₂ and temperature |
| **Endocrine** | Addison's disease, Cushing's (psychosis), phaeochromocytoma |

**Delirium vs psychosis:** Delirium = fluctuating consciousness, disorientation, inattention (cannot perform serial 7s or months backwards), usually has organic cause, typically in elderly or medically unwell. Psychosis = clear consciousness, formal thought disorder, auditory hallucinations, delusions, insight impaired.

### Suicide Risk — Neurobiology

Suicidal behaviour involves dysregulation of serotonergic systems (reduced 5-HT activity in prefrontal cortex → impaired impulse control), HPA-axis hyperactivity (stress response dysregulation), and reduced frontal lobe inhibition. Key risk factor interaction: chronic risk (psychiatric diagnosis, previous attempt, family history, social isolation) + acute precipitants (loss, intoxication, crisis) = crisis state.

### Rapid Tranquilisation — Pharmacology

**Goal:** Reduce distress and prevent harm to patient and others — NOT chemical restraint for staff convenience. The aim is **calm and cooperative**, not unconscious.

**Oral route first (if patient accepts):**
- Lorazepam 1–2 mg oral/sublingual (benzodiazepine — GABA-A agonist → sedation)
- Olanzapine 5–10 mg oral (antipsychotic — D2/5-HT2A blockade → reduces psychotic agitation)
- **Do NOT combine IM olanzapine with IM/IV benzodiazepines** — fatal respiratory depression risk (black box warning; deaths reported)

**IM route (if oral refused or patient too agitated):**
- **Lorazepam 1–2 mg IM + haloperidol 5 mg IM** — evidence-based first-line RT combination (TROVE/SHTAP guidelines)
- **IM olanzapine 10 mg IM** — effective; do not give with benzodiazepines in the same session
- **Promethazine 25–50 mg IM** — antihistamine sedative; can be combined with haloperidol; preferred when benzodiazepine respiratory risk is a concern (TRASA trial: haloperidol + promethazine vs haloperidol alone — promethazine combination superior)

**Monitoring after RT:** 15-minutely BP, HR, SpO₂, RR, and GCS for 1 hour post-administration. Flumazenil available if benzodiazepine overdose causes respiratory depression.

## Clinical Presentation

### Suicide Risk Assessment — Structured Approach

**SAD PERSONS score (mnemonic for risk factors):**
- **S** — Sex (male — higher completed suicide)
- **A** — Age (<19 or >45)
- **D** — Depression (or any mental illness)
- **P** — Previous attempt (strongest predictor of future attempt)
- **E** — Ethanol/substance abuse
- **R** — Rational thinking loss (psychosis, delusions)
- **S** — Social supports lacking
- **O** — Organised or serious plan
- **N** — No significant other
- **S** — Sickness (chronic medical illness)

**Structured tools:** Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) — categorises ideation (passive wish to die → active ideation without plan → active ideation with plan → intent to act) and behaviour (preparatory acts → interrupted attempt → actual attempt). More reliable than clinical intuition alone.

### Acute Psychosis Presentation

- **Positive symptoms:** Hallucinations (most commonly auditory — voices commenting, commanding), delusions (persecutory, grandiose, reference), thought disorder (loose associations, tangentiality, word salad), thought insertion/withdrawal/broadcasting
- **Negative symptoms:** Alogia (poverty of speech), anhedonia, avolition, flat affect, social withdrawal
- **First episode psychosis (FEP):** Schizophrenia, affective psychosis (bipolar), drug-induced, organic
- **Drug-induced psychosis:** Cannabis (especially high-potency/skunk) → psychosis indistinguishable from schizophrenia; cocaine/amphetamines → paranoid psychosis

### Mental Health Act — Key Sections

| Section | Power | Who | Duration | Purpose |
|---------|-------|-----|----------|---------|
| **Section 136** | Police remove from public place to place of safety | Police alone | 24h (extendable to 36h with approval) | Assessment by approved clinician + AMHP |
| **Section 2** | Admission for assessment | 2 doctors (one s12 approved) + AMHP | 28 days | Assessment ± treatment |
| **Section 3** | Admission for treatment | 2 doctors (one s12 approved) + AMHP | 6 months (renewable) | Treatment of known mental disorder |
| **Section 4** | Emergency admission (1 doctor) | 1 doctor + AMHP | 72h | Where Section 2 criteria met but only 1 doctor available |
| **Section 5(2)** | Doctor's holding power | Registered medical practitioner | 72h | Detain inpatient who wishes to leave |
| **Section 5(4)** | Nurse's holding power | Registered mental health nurse | 6h | Detain inpatient while doctor sought |

**Section 136:** Police find person in a public place appearing to have a mental disorder who is in immediate need of care/control → take to "place of safety" (usually ED or designated health-based place of safety). Not a conviction; does not appear on criminal record. Must be assessed by approved clinician (AC) + approved mental health professional (AMHP) within 24 hours.

### Mental Capacity Act 2005 — The 4 Elements

Capacity is **decision-specific** and **time-specific** — a patient may lack capacity for one decision but retain it for another.

**A person lacks capacity if they are unable to:**
1. **Understand** the information relevant to the decision
2. **Retain** that information long enough to make the decision
3. **Use or weigh** that information as part of the decision-making process
4. **Communicate** their decision (by any means)

**Presumption of capacity:** All adults are presumed to have capacity unless proven otherwise. Capacity assessment must be documented for every significant decision where capacity is in doubt.

**Best interests decision:** If capacity absent → act in patient's best interests; consider previously expressed wishes (advance decisions, lasting power of attorney); involve family/carers as consultees; least restrictive option principle.

## Diagnosis

### Systematic Organic Exclusion — The "Medical Clearance"

Every patient presenting with acute behavioural disturbance in A&E requires organic exclusion:

**Mandatory:**
- BM (glucose): hypoglycaemia is the most common and treatable organic cause of acute confusion
- SpO₂
- Temperature
- HR, BP
- ECG (QTc prolongation baseline before antipsychotics; Brugada in cocaine use)

**As indicated:**
- FBC, U&E, LFTs, TFTs, CRP
- ABG (hypoxia, hypercapnia)
- Urine drug screen (UDS) and serum ethanol
- CT head (new-onset psychosis, focal neurology, significant head injury, anticoagulated)
- Urine/blood cultures if sepsis suspected
- Anti-NMDA receptor antibodies (young women with new psychosis + movement disorder)
- **LP:** If encephalitis or SAH suspected

### When to Suspect Organic Cause

- **First episode psychosis** in older patient (>40) or no family history
- **Visual hallucinations** (more organic than auditory)
- **Fluctuating consciousness** → delirium, not psychosis
- **Focal neurological signs**
- **Fever/neck stiffness** → meningitis/encephalitis
- **Autonomic instability** → NMS, serotonin syndrome, stimulant toxicity
- **Positive drug screen** — but be cautious; substance intoxication may coexist with, not cause, psychosis

## Management

### Immediate Safety — De-escalation First

**Verbal de-escalation before medication:**
- Calm, non-threatening tone; introduce yourself; maintain safe distance (personal space ~1.5m)
- Move patient to quiet area (sensory reduction reduces agitation)
- Offer choice (drink, sitting vs standing, oral medication)
- Acknowledge distress without challenging beliefs ("I can see you're frightened" not "there are no voices")
- Avoid restraint until all verbal options exhausted

**Physical restraint:** If patient is an immediate danger to self or others → minimum necessary force; must be documented with justification; debrief patient and staff after.

### Rapid Tranquilisation Protocol

**Step 1 — Oral (offer first):**
- Lorazepam 1–2 mg oral + olanzapine 5–10 mg oral (do NOT give IM olanzapine if oral benzo given recently)
- Or lorazepam alone if antipsychotic contraindicated

**Step 2 — IM (if oral refused or insufficient):**
- **Lorazepam 1–2 mg IM + haloperidol 5 mg IM** (standard combination)
- OR IM olanzapine 10 mg (not within 1 hour of IM benzodiazepine)
- Repeat after 30–45 minutes if insufficient effect; maximum doses per session per local protocol
- **Elderly or medically compromised:** Halve doses; use lorazepam alone or low-dose haloperidol

**Step 3 — IV sedation (anaesthetic involvement):**
- Midazolam IV (short-acting benzodiazepine) for severe refractory agitation
- Anaesthetic/ICU team must be present — airway management immediately available

**Monitoring after RT:** SpO₂, RR, HR, BP, GCS every 15 minutes. Airway adjuncts and flumazenil/naloxone immediately available.

### Suicide Risk — Management Decision

**High risk (active plan, intent, access to means, psychosis, previous high-lethality attempt):**
- 1:1 nursing observation immediately
- Psychiatric liaison/on-call psychiatry urgent assessment
- Consideration for inpatient admission — voluntary or under MHA Section 2/3

**Medium risk (ideation without plan, chronic risk with acute precipitant):**
- Psychiatric liaison assessment
- Crisis plan developed with patient
- Community crisis team involvement
- Safety planning (identify warning signs, coping strategies, emergency contacts)

**If patient attempts to leave before assessment:**
- If capacity present and risk assessed as manageable → difficult legal situation; consider MHA
- If capacity absent (drug/alcohol, acute psychosis, severe depression impairing judgment) → MCA best interests detainment until capacity regained
- **Section 5(2):** Consultant or senior registrar can invoke to hold inpatient for 72h

### NMS and Serotonin Syndrome — Recognition

**Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Dopamine receptor blockade by antipsychotics → Fever + rigidity (lead-pipe) + altered consciousness + autonomic instability (labile BP, tachycardia, diaphoresis). **Stop antipsychotic immediately; dantrolene for muscle rigidity; bromocriptine (dopamine agonist); ITU.**

**Serotonin Syndrome:** Excess serotonergic activity (SSRI + tramadol, SSRI + MAOI, SSRI + linezolid). Hunter criteria: clonus (inducible/spontaneous/ocular) + agitation + diaphoresis + tremor + hyperreflexia. **Stop serotonergic agent; cyproheptadine (5-HT2 antagonist); benzodiazepines for agitation; ITU if severe.**

## Complications

- **Respiratory depression from RT:** Particularly benzodiazepines ± alcohol/opioids; SpO₂ monitoring mandatory; flumazenil available
- **Aspiration:** During physical restraint in prone position → AVOID prone restraint
- **Positional asphyxia:** Restraint in face-down position restricts breathing — has caused deaths; restraint in supine or seated position; never sit or kneel on patient's chest
- **QTc prolongation:** Haloperidol + droperidol prolong QTc → torsades de pointes; baseline ECG; avoid if QTc >500 ms
- **Metabolic syndrome:** Antipsychotics (especially olanzapine, clozapine) → weight gain, insulin resistance, hyperlipidaemia — monitoring programme required for all patients on antipsychotics
- **Clozapine agranulocytosis:** Neutropenia in 1–3% of clozapine users; mandatory haematological monitoring (weekly for 18 weeks, fortnightly for 1 year, then monthly); immediate withdrawal if neutrophils <1.5 × 10⁹/L

## Clinical Insight

The single most important clinical principle in A&E psychiatric emergencies is: **organic cause first, always**. A patient presenting with apparent psychosis who is actually hypoglycaemic, hypoxic, or in non-convulsive status epilepticus will deteriorate if given antipsychotics and sent to a psychiatric holding area without the cause being treated. The BM is the first investigation in every confused or agitated patient — a 30-second test that is still missed far too often.

Rapid tranquilisation generates significant anxiety among junior doctors, particularly around the combination of drugs. The critical safety rule is the **IM olanzapine + benzodiazepine interaction**: combining IM olanzapine with IM/IV benzodiazepines has caused deaths from cardiorespiratory depression. They should not be given within 1 hour of each other. The safe combination is **IM lorazepam + IM haloperidol** — this combination is well-studied, effective, and does not carry the same risk. If the patient has taken oral lorazepam (much slower absorption and lower peak), the risk is lower, but caution is still warranted.

The Mental Capacity Act is frequently confused with the Mental Health Act. The distinction is conceptually important: **MCA** applies to patients who lack capacity to make a specific decision for any reason (including acutely — intoxication, delirium, unconsciousness) — it permits treatment in best interests for physical and mental health conditions. **MHA** applies specifically to patients with mental disorder who require assessment or treatment — it can be used even against a capacitous patient's wishes, which is its unique power. In A&E, the practical question is often: "Can I keep this patient here?" The answer depends on whether they lack capacity (MCA → yes, best interests), or have capacity but mental disorder warranting detention (MHA Section 2/3 needed — requires two doctors and AMHP).

## Scenario Walkthrough

**The patient:** 32M, Section 136, on bridge with intent to jump, agitated/threatening in A&E, schizophrenia, not engaged with CMHT, UDS positive cannabis + cocaine.

**Step 1 — Immediate safety:**
- Place patient in a single room away from others; 1:1 nurse observation
- Remove potential weapons/ligature risks from environment
- Alert security; ensure staff safety while maintaining therapeutic engagement

**Step 2 — De-escalation:**
- Calm approach; introduce yourself; non-confrontational positioning
- Offer drink/seat; acknowledge he must be in a lot of distress
- Do not challenge delusions or argue about the bridge

**Step 3 — Organic exclusion (simultaneously with de-escalation):**
- **BM immediately** — cannabis + cocaine = metabolic risk; rule out hypoglycaemia
- SpO₂, temperature, HR, BP
- ECG — cocaine causes coronary spasm and QTc prolongation
- UDS confirmed; serum ethanol
- If settles enough for examination: pupils (cocaine = dilated; opioids = pinpoint), tone, reflexes

**Step 4 — Rapid tranquilisation (if de-escalation fails):**
- Offer oral lorazepam 1–2 mg; if refuses → **IM lorazepam 2 mg + IM haloperidol 5 mg**
- Post-RT monitoring every 15 minutes
- Do NOT give IM olanzapine given recent cocaine use (QTc risk)

**Step 5 — Capacity and Mental Health Act:**
- Patient came under Section 136 → must be assessed by AC + AMHP within 24h
- Can he make a capacitous decision about admission? Drug intoxication + acute psychosis likely impairs capacity currently
- Inform psychiatric liaison team immediately
- If patient attempts to leave before assessment → Section 5(2) by on-call doctor

**Step 6 — Suicide risk (after organic causes treated/excluded):**
- Formal risk assessment: clear intent (stated plan, on bridge), psychosis, substance use, poor social support, non-adherent with medication → HIGH RISK
- **Admission under MHA Section 2** likely appropriate pending formal assessment by AMHP + s12 doctor

**Exam trigger:** *Agitated psychotic patient* → de-escalate first, offer oral medication, then IM lorazepam + haloperidol if needed. NEVER give IM olanzapine + IM benzodiazepine together. Always exclude organic causes (BM/SpO₂/temperature) before attributing to psychiatric illness.
