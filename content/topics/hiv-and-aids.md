## Overview

HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) is a single-stranded RNA retrovirus that targets CD4+ T lymphocytes, progressively depleting cellular immunity. Untreated, it leads to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) — defined as a CD4 count <200 cells/µL or the occurrence of an AIDS-defining illness. The global epidemic affects over 38 million people. In the era of combination antiretroviral therapy (cART), HIV is a manageable chronic condition — people diagnosed today and started on treatment have near-normal life expectancy. The tragedy of HIV mortality now lies almost entirely in late diagnosis.

## Pathophysiology

HIV-1 (responsible for the global epidemic; HIV-2 is endemic in West Africa, less virulent) infects cells expressing the CD4 receptor — primarily CD4+ T helper lymphocytes, but also macrophages, dendritic cells, and microglia.

**Entry mechanism**: gp120 on the viral envelope binds to CD4, then to a co-receptor (CCR5 for macrophage-tropic strains — important early in infection; CXCR4 for T-cell tropic strains — important in late disease). Inhibiting CCR5 (maraviroc) or CD4/gp120 binding (ibalizumab) are therapeutic targets.

After entry, the viral RNA is reverse-transcribed into DNA by **reverse transcriptase** (an error-prone, RNA-dependent DNA polymerase — source of high mutation rate and drug resistance). The DNA is integrated into the host genome by **integrase**. Viral proteins are translated as a polyprotein, which is cleaved by **protease** into functional units. Each of these steps — reverse transcription, integration, and protease activity — is targeted by antiretroviral drugs.

Progressive CD4 cell depletion:
- CD4 >500: generally well; may have minor mucocutaneous manifestations
- CD4 200–500: increased susceptibility to bacterial infections, oral candidiasis, seborrhoeic dermatitis, recurrent herpes
- CD4 <200: AIDS-defining illness threshold; PCP, toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal meningitis
- CD4 <100: CMV retinitis, MAC (Mycobacterium avium complex)
- CD4 <50: Severe CMV disease, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML)

## Clinical Presentation

### Primary HIV Infection (Seroconversion Illness)

Occurs 2–6 weeks after transmission. A mononucleosis-like syndrome: fever, pharyngitis, lymphadenopathy, maculopapular rash (trunk and face), myalgia, arthralgia, headache, diarrhoea. Often dismissed as "viral illness." Extremely high viral load during this period — maximally infectious. Diagnosable with HIV RNA PCR (antibody tests may be negative for up to 45 days — the "window period").

### AIDS-Defining Illnesses by CD4 Count

**CD4 <200:**

**Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP)** — most common AIDS-defining illness in developed countries. Insidious onset progressive dyspnoea, dry cough, and fever over weeks. CXR: bilateral perihilar ground-glass infiltrates (can be normal early). LDH elevated — a sensitive but non-specific marker of severity. PaO₂ often severely reduced. Diagnosis: induced sputum or BAL with Grocott (GMS) stain. Treatment: high-dose co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX) for 21 days. Add prednisolone if PaO₂ <70 mmHg (reduces mortality).

**CD4 <100:**

**Toxoplasmosis encephalitis** — ring-enhancing lesions on CT/MRI, multiple, predominantly in the basal ganglia and cortex. Headache, fever, focal neurological deficits, seizures. Treatment: pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folinic acid.

**Cryptococcal meningitis** — subacute meningitis in a patient from Africa or Asia (high burden). Headache, fever, neck stiffness (may be absent). CSF: India ink stain positive (encapsulated yeast); elevated opening pressure; cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) highly sensitive. Treatment: IV amphotericin B + flucytosine (induction, 2 weeks) → fluconazole (consolidation, then maintenance). Critical: **lumbar puncture pressure management** — elevated ICP causes blindness and death; repeated LP drainage or lumbar drain.

**CMV retinitis** — painless, progressive visual field loss or "floaters." Fundoscopy: "pizza-pie" appearance — haemorrhages, exudates, necrosis along the vascular arcades. Treatment: valganciclovir (oral) or IV ganciclovir for severe disease.

**CD4 <50:**

**Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC)** — disseminated disease with fever, night sweats, weight loss, diarrhoea, hepatosplenomegaly, and markedly elevated ALP. Blood cultures in specific mycobacterial bottles. Treatment: clarithromycin + ethambutol ± rifabutin.

**Other AIDS-defining illnesses**: Oesophageal candidiasis (odynophagia — distinct from oral thrush), Kaposi's sarcoma (HHV-8 — violaceous skin lesions), CNS lymphoma (primary — single or multiple ring-enhancing lesions, solitary, in contrast to toxoplasmosis which is usually multiple), Wasting syndrome, HIV encephalopathy.

### AIDS-Defining Conditions — Complete List (First Aid High-Yield)

CDC-defined conditions that diagnose AIDS regardless of CD4 count (the presence of any one = AIDS):

| Category | Conditions |
|---------|-----------|
| **Infections** | PCP (*Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia), Toxoplasmosis of brain, Cryptococcosis (extrapulmonary), CMV disease (other than liver/spleen/nodes), CMV retinitis, Histoplasmosis (disseminated/extrapulmonary), Coccidioidomycosis (disseminated), MAC (disseminated or extrapulmonary), TB (pulmonary or extrapulmonary), Recurrent bacterial pneumonia (≥2 episodes/year), Recurrent *Salmonella* septicaemia, Chronic intestinal cryptosporidiosis (>1 month), Chronic isosporiasis (>1 month), Chronic mucocutaneous HSV (>1 month or bronchitis/oesophagitis/pneumonitis) |
| **Malignancies** | Kaposi's sarcoma (HHV-8), Burkitt's lymphoma (or equivalent term), Immunoblastic lymphoma (or equivalent), Primary CNS lymphoma (Epstein-Barr associated), Invasive cervical cancer |
| **Other** | HIV encephalopathy (AIDS dementia complex), Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML — JC virus), HIV wasting syndrome (>10% body weight loss + chronic diarrhoea or fever), Oesophageal candidiasis |

**FA buzzwords by CD4 threshold:**
- **CD4 <500**: Oral candidiasis (thrush), hairy leukoplakia (EBV — on lateral tongue; doesn't scrape off unlike candida), seborrhoeic dermatitis, VZV reactivation, recurrent herpes, CIN/cervical dysplasia
- **CD4 <200**: PCP → start prophylaxis at this threshold (co-trimoxazole)
- **CD4 <100**: Toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal meningitis (from pigeon droppings — *Cryptococcus* encapsulated by India ink)
- **CD4 <50**: CMV retinitis, disseminated MAC (fever + night sweats + weight loss + elevated ALP + anaemia), PML (JC virus — progressive neurological decline, white matter lesions, no mass effect, no enhancement)

**Kaposi's sarcoma (KS)**: HHV-8 (Human Herpesvirus 8). Violaceous (purple-red), non-tender, non-pruritic plaques/nodules on the skin, mucous membranes, and viscera. KS is the most common AIDS-associated malignancy. Treatment: local therapies for limited disease; systemic pegylated liposomal doxorubicin; control depends critically on ART — immune reconstitution often causes KS regression.

**Hairy leukoplakia**: White, corrugated (hairy) lesion on the lateral border of the tongue — EBV-driven. Unlike oral candidiasis, **cannot be scraped off**. Not premalignant. Resolves with ART.

## Diagnosis

**HIV antibody/antigen test (4th generation)**: Detects both HIV p24 antigen (positive from day 16 of infection) and IgG antibody (positive by day 45). The standard screening test. Sensitivity >99.9%. A reactive result requires confirmation with an HIV-1/2 differentiation assay.

**HIV RNA PCR (viral load)**: Detects viral RNA directly — used in the window period when antibody tests may be negative; confirms diagnosis; used for monitoring response to therapy. Quantitative (copies/mL).

**CD4 count**: Reflects degree of immunosuppression; guides prophylaxis decisions; monitors immune reconstitution on therapy.

**Baseline investigations at diagnosis:**
- Full blood count, LFTs, U&E, glucose, lipids (baseline before ART)
- HIV genotype resistance testing — before starting ART
- Hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C antibody, hepatitis A IgG
- Syphilis serology (TPPA/VDRL)
- Toxoplasma IgG (determines risk and need for prophylaxis)
- CMV IgG
- Cervical smear (women — increased CIN risk)
- TB screen (IGRA or Mantoux, CXR)
## Management

### Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)

**Start immediately** — regardless of CD4 count. ART reduces viral load to undetectable (<50 copies/mL), restores immune function, prevents AIDS-defining illnesses, and prevents HIV transmission. **U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable** — a person on ART with an undetectable viral load cannot sexually transmit HIV.

**Current preferred first-line regimen (BHIVA 2023):**
Two NRTIs + one integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI):
- **Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) / emtricitabine (FTC) + bictegravir** (single tablet regimen — once daily)
- **Abacavir / lamivudine + dolutegravir** — if HLA-B*5701 negative (abacavir hypersensitivity — screen before prescribing)

**Drug classes:**

| Class | Mechanism | Examples |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| NRTI (nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor) | Competitive inhibition of reverse transcriptase | Tenofovir, emtricitabine, abacavir, lamivudine |
| NNRTI (non-nucleoside RTI) | Non-competitive allosteric inhibition | Efavirenz, rilpivirine, doravirine |
| INSTI (integrase strand transfer inhibitor) | Block viral DNA integration | Dolutegravir, bictegravir, raltegravir |
| PI (protease inhibitor) | Block polyprotein cleavage | Darunavir (boosted with ritonavir or cobicistat) |
| Entry inhibitor | Block CD4/CCR5 binding | Maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist), ibalizumab |

**Monitoring on ART**: Viral load at 1 month (should be falling), 3 months (should be <200 copies/mL), then every 6 months when suppressed. CD4 count 3–6 monthly until >350, then annually.

**Treatment failure**: Viral load >200 copies/mL on two consecutive measurements on ART. Causes: non-adherence (most common), resistance. HIV genotype to guide regimen change.

### OI Prophylaxis

| CD4 | Prophylaxis |
|-----|------------|
| <200 | Co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX) — prevents PCP and toxoplasmosis |
| <100 | Co-trimoxazole covers toxoplasmosis (or add pyrimethamine) |
| <50 | Azithromycin weekly — MAC prophylaxis |

Prophylaxis can be discontinued when CD4 rises consistently above threshold on ART.

### Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)

Daily oral tenofovir-DF/emtricitabine (or event-based dosing) for HIV-negative individuals at substantial risk of HIV acquisition. Reduces transmission risk by >99% when used correctly. Available on NHS England since 2020.

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

Start within 72 hours of exposure (ideally within 24 hours). 28-day course of two NRTIs + one INSTI (e.g., tenofovir/emtricitabine + raltegravir). Occupational and non-occupational exposures.

## Complications

**Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS)**: Paradoxical worsening of a known or unmasked opportunistic infection 2–8 weeks after starting ART, as recovering immunity mounts an exuberant inflammatory response. Common with TB, MAC, CMV, and cryptococcal meningitis. Manage by continuing ART and treating the OI; steroids if severe.

**Metabolic complications of ART**: Dyslipidaemia (especially PIs and older NRTIs), insulin resistance, lipodystrophy (lipoatrophy + central fat accumulation), osteoporosis. Renal toxicity (tenofovir disoproxil fumarate — TDF — more nephrotoxic than TAF). Cardiovascular risk is elevated in HIV itself (chronic inflammation) and compounded by some drugs.

**Non-AIDS malignancies**: Significantly elevated risk of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, anal cancer (HPV-related — screen with anal Pap smears), lung cancer, liver cancer (from viral hepatitis coinfection), Kaposi's sarcoma (even at higher CD4 counts in some patients).

**Coinfections**: Hepatitis B (treat with tenofovir-containing ART — tenofovir is active against HBV), hepatitis C (highly effective DAA therapy — cure rates >95%), TB (TB is both the most common OI globally and the most common cause of death in HIV-positive people worldwide).

## Clinical Insight

Late diagnosis is the greatest driver of HIV mortality and morbidity in the UK. The median CD4 count at diagnosis is still below 350 in many centres — meaning patients are diagnosed at a point where immune damage has already occurred. Routine opt-out HIV testing in all new GP registrations, all medical admissions, and all sexual health attendances is BHIVA-endorsed and clinically essential. Every clinician in every specialty has a role in HIV testing.

IRIS following ART initiation in patients with active cryptococcal meningitis is a genuine risk — the reconstituting immune system can cause fatal raised intracranial pressure. Current guidance recommends delaying ART for 4–6 weeks after starting treatment for cryptococcal meningitis, once the patient is clinically stable, to reduce IRIS risk. This is one of the few scenarios where immediate ART is not recommended.

U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable) is one of the most transformative concepts in infectious disease. A person with HIV on effective ART with a consistently undetectable viral load has zero risk of transmitting HIV sexually. This message is essential for reducing stigma, promoting testing, and supporting treatment adherence — and it is now supported by the highest level of evidence (PARTNER and HPTN 052 studies).

## Scenario Walkthrough

**The patient:** A 34-year-old man with 4 weeks of fever, night sweats, 8 kg weight loss, and non-productive cough. White plaques on tongue that **bleed when scraped**. CD4 62 cells/µL, viral load 340,000 copies/mL. CXR: bilateral diffuse ground-glass infiltrates.

**Step 1 — Diagnose AIDS and identify the infections:**
- CD4 **62 cells/µL** = **AIDS** (CD4 <200 cells/µL = AIDS-defining)
- White plaques that bleed when scraped = **oral candidiasis** (distinguishes from oral hairy leukoplakia, which does not bleed or scrape off and is caused by EBV)
- Bilateral ground-glass infiltrates + non-productive cough + CD4 <200 = **Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP)** until proven otherwise

**Step 2 — Confirm PCP:**
- ABG: PaO₂ characteristically disproportionately low; calculate A-a gradient (elevated)
- **LDH** — elevated in PCP; correlates with severity (LDH >500 = severe disease)
- **Bronchoscopy + BAL** for Gomori methenamine silver (GMS) stain — confirms *P. jirovecii* cysts. High-sensitivity PCR on BAL increasingly used.
- β-D-glucan: elevated in PCP; useful if BAL not immediately available

**Step 3 — Treat PCP immediately:**
- **High-dose co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX)** — IV if severe (PaO₂ <70 mmHg), oral if mild-moderate. Standard dose: 15–20 mg/kg/day (TMP component) in 3–4 divided doses × 21 days
- **Add prednisolone 40 mg BD** if PaO₂ <70 mmHg or A-a gradient >35 mmHg — reduces respiratory failure and mortality. Start within 24–72 hours of antibiotics.
- Alternative if TMP-SMX intolerant: IV pentamidine

**Step 4 — When to start ART:** Not immediately.
- For **PCP**: start ART **within 2 weeks** of starting PCP treatment (ACTG 5164 trial) — early ART reduces mortality; but unlike cryptococcal meningitis, the IRIS risk is lower with PCP
- For **cryptococcal meningitis**: delay ART 4–6 weeks (IRIS causes fatal raised ICP)

**Step 5 — Ongoing prophylaxis (once stable on ART):**
- Continue co-trimoxazole prophylaxis until CD4 >200 cells/µL for ≥3 months
- CD4 <100: also prophylaxis for toxoplasmosis (co-trimoxazole covers both)
- CD4 <50: MAC prophylaxis (azithromycin 1250 mg weekly)

**Exam trigger:** *CD4 <200 + non-productive cough + bilateral ground-glass infiltrates* → PCP. *White tongue plaques that bleed when scraped* → candidiasis (not leukoplakia). Co-trimoxazole + steroids if PaO₂ <70 mmHg. Do not start ART simultaneously with PCP treatment — wait 2 weeks.
