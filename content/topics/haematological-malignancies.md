## Overview

Haematological malignancies are cancers of blood-forming and lymphoid tissue. They are broadly classified by cell of origin (myeloid vs lymphoid) and behaviour (acute vs chronic). Acute leukaemias are rapidly fatal without treatment; chronic leukaemias and lymphomas typically have more indolent courses but are heterogeneous. The presenting triad of anaemia, infection susceptibility, and bleeding reflects failure of normal haematopoiesis — either from bone marrow infiltration or from the production of dysfunctional cells.

## Pathophysiology

### Acute Leukaemias

**Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML)**: Clonal proliferation of myeloid blasts arrested at an early stage of differentiation, accumulating in the bone marrow and suppressing normal haematopoiesis. Defined by ≥20% blasts in the bone marrow (WHO criteria). Auer rods — pink cytoplasmic needle-like inclusions — are pathognomonic for AML on blood film; their presence in a blast is diagnostic. Cytogenetics are critical for prognosis: t(15;17) (APL — acute promyelocytic leukaemia) is the most important subtype because it is uniquely sensitive to all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) and arsenic trioxide.

**Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL)**: Clonal proliferation of lymphoid precursors (B-cell or T-cell). The most common malignancy of childhood. Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) (BCR-ABL1) is present in ~25% of adult ALL and confers poor prognosis — requires imatinib-containing regimen. CNS sanctuary is a unique feature of ALL — leukaemic blasts cross the blood-brain barrier; intrathecal chemotherapy and/or cranial irradiation are required.

### Chronic Leukaemias

**Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)**: Defined by the **Philadelphia chromosome** — t(9;22) — which creates the BCR-ABL1 fusion gene. BCR-ABL1 is a constitutively active tyrosine kinase that drives uncontrolled myeloid proliferation. This molecular target led directly to the development of imatinib (the first targeted cancer therapy), which transformed CML from a uniformly fatal disease to one where most patients achieve normal life expectancy.

CML progresses through three phases: chronic phase (indolent, responds to TKI) → accelerated phase → blast crisis (transformation to acute leukaemia, median survival months without stem cell transplant).

**Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)**: The most common leukaemia in adults in Western countries. Clonal expansion of mature-appearing but functionally incompetent B-lymphocytes (CD5+/CD19+/CD23+). Smear (smudge) cells on blood film are characteristic — fragile abnormal lymphocytes that smear on preparation. Most patients are diagnosed incidentally on a routine blood count.

### Lymphomas

**Hodgkin lymphoma (HL)**: Characterised by the pathognomonic **Reed-Sternberg (RS) cell** — a large binucleated cell with prominent "owl-eye" nucleoli, surrounded by a reactive inflammatory infiltrate. RS cells are derived from germinal centre B-cells. HL is unique among lymphomas in its highly predictable pattern of spread (contiguous lymph node groups) and its exceptional curability even in advanced stages.

**Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)**: A heterogeneous group of lymphoid malignancies. The most important distinction is indolent vs aggressive:
- **Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)** — most common aggressive NHL; rapidly fatal without treatment but potentially curable with R-CHOP
- **Follicular lymphoma** — most common indolent NHL; not curable with standard therapy but very long natural history; "watch and wait" appropriate in asymptomatic disease
- **Burkitt's lymphoma** — highly aggressive; t(8;14) translocation (MYC oncogene); "starry sky" pattern on histology; associated with EBV; endemic (jaw) and sporadic (abdomen) forms

### Myeloma

**Multiple myeloma**: Malignant proliferation of plasma cells in the bone marrow, producing a monoclonal immunoglobulin (paraprotein). Plasma cells produce osteoclast-activating factors → lytic bone lesions and hypercalcaemia. Monoclonal light chains (Bence-Jones proteins) are filtered by the glomerulus and damage the tubules → cast nephropathy → renal failure.

**MGUS** (monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance) → smouldering myeloma → symptomatic myeloma: a spectrum. MGUS progresses to myeloma at ~1% per year. Smouldering myeloma at ~10% per year.

## Clinical Presentation

### The Bone Marrow Failure Triad

Marrow infiltration suppresses normal haematopoiesis, producing:
- **Anaemia**: fatigue, pallor, dyspnoea
- **Neutropaenia**: recurrent, severe, or unusual infections
- **Thrombocytopaenia**: spontaneous bruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding

### Disease-Specific Features

**AML**: Acute onset over days to weeks. Gum hypertrophy and skin infiltration (chloromas) in monocytic subtypes. APL presents with severe coagulopathy (DIC) — high mortality from haemorrhage. **Leukostasis** — hyperviscosity from extremely high blast count (WBC >100 × 10⁹/L) — causes respiratory failure, stroke, and priapism.

**CML**: Often incidentally discovered on FBC. Massive splenomegaly (can be enormous), leukocytosis with the full myeloid spectrum (from blasts to mature neutrophils — the "left shift" across all stages), elevated basophils (characteristic).

**CLL**: Painless lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, recurrent infections (hypogammaglobulinaemia — dysfunctional B-cells cannot produce normal antibody). **Richter's transformation** — transformation to DLBCL — presents as sudden deterioration, fever, and rapid lymph node enlargement.

**Hodgkin lymphoma**: Young adults (bimodal peak 15–35 and >55 years). Painless cervical lymphadenopathy. **B symptoms** — fever (>38°C), drenching night sweats, weight loss >10% in 6 months — indicate systemic disease and adverse prognosis. **Pel-Ebstein fever** — cyclical high fever alternating with afebrile periods (classic but uncommon). Alcohol-induced pain in lymph nodes — pathognomonic but rare.

**Multiple myeloma** — the CRAB criteria for symptomatic disease:
- **C**alcium elevated (>2.75 mmol/L)
- **R**enal impairment (creatinine >177 µmol/L or eGFR <40)
- **A**naemia (Hb <100 g/L)
- **B**one lesions (lytic lesions, osteoporosis, pathological fractures)

Bone pain (especially back pain from vertebral collapse) is the most common presenting symptom. Hyperviscosity from paraprotein: headache, visual disturbance, confusion, bleeding.

## Diagnosis

**Blood count and film**: The most important initial investigation. Blasts, smear cells, Auer rods, and abnormal differential are all diagnostic clues.

**Bone marrow biopsy**: Trephine (core biopsy for architecture) and aspirate (for cytogenetics, flow cytometry) are complementary. Essential for diagnosing leukaemia and myeloma, staging lymphoma, and assessing treatment response.

**Immunophenotyping (flow cytometry)**: Identifies cell surface markers to classify the malignancy — CLL (CD5+/CD19+/CD23+), ALL (TdT+/CD10+), AML (CD13/33/117+).

**Cytogenetics and FISH**: Critical for prognosis and treatment selection. Philadelphia chromosome, t(15;17), t(8;14), del(17p) in CLL — each carries specific treatment implications.

**Lymph node biopsy**: Excision biopsy (not fine needle aspiration — architecture is required) for lymphoma diagnosis. RS cells confirm HL; large cells with pleomorphic nuclei confirm DLBCL.

**Protein electrophoresis (SPEP/UPEP)**: Identifies the paraprotein band (M-band) in myeloma. Quantitative immunoglobulins, free light chains. Bence-Jones protein (free light chains in urine) detected on UPEP.

**Staging — Ann Arbor for lymphoma**:

| Stage | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| I | Single lymph node region or single extralymphatic site |
| II | Two or more regions, same side of the diaphragm |
| III | Both sides of the diaphragm |
| IV | Diffuse extralymphatic involvement (bone marrow, liver, lung) |

A = no B symptoms; B = fever, night sweats, weight loss.

**CT chest/abdomen/pelvis + PET-CT**: Staging in lymphoma. PET is superior to CT for metabolically active disease and is used to assess treatment response (Deauville score after 2 cycles).
## Management

### Acute Leukaemia

**AML**: Induction chemotherapy with cytarabine + anthracycline (7+3 regimen — 7 days cytarabine infusion + 3 days daunorubicin). Consolidation with high-dose cytarabine or allogeneic stem cell transplant (alloSCT) in high-risk disease. **APL is treated with ATRA + arsenic trioxide** — targeted therapy that induces differentiation of promyelocytes; highly curative even in advanced disease. Manage DIC aggressively with FFP and cryoprecipitate.

**ALL**: Multi-agent induction (vincristine, dexamethasone, asparaginase ± anthracycline), CNS prophylaxis, consolidation, and 2 years maintenance. Philadelphia+ ALL: imatinib or dasatinib added to chemotherapy. Allogeneic SCT for high-risk or relapsed disease. CD19-directed CAR-T cell therapy (tisagenlecleucel) for relapsed/refractory B-ALL.

### Chronic Leukaemia

**CML**: **Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)** — imatinib is first-line (generic, cheap, highly effective); dasatinib or nilotinib as second-line or for specific BCR-ABL mutations. Target: complete molecular response (BCR-ABL1 transcript undetectable by PCR). Most patients require lifelong treatment; treatment-free remission is achievable in ~40% who sustain deep molecular response.

**CLL**: "Watch and wait" for asymptomatic stage A disease — treatment does not improve survival in early disease. Treat when symptomatic (Binet/Rai staging thresholds), rapidly progressing, or with cytopaenias. First-line: **ibrutinib** (BTK inhibitor) or **venetoclax + obinutuzumab** — these have largely replaced FCR (fludarabine, cyclophosphamide, rituximab) chemoimmunotherapy. TP53 mutation/del(17p) confers resistance to chemotherapy — these patients require targeted therapy.

### Lymphoma

**Hodgkin lymphoma**: ABVD chemotherapy (doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, dacarbazine) ± radiotherapy. PET-adapted therapy — escalate or de-escalate based on interim PET response (Deauville score). Advanced HL: BEACOPP (more intensive, more toxic). **Cure rates >90% for early-stage HL; ~80% for advanced HL** — one of the great oncological success stories.

**DLBCL**: R-CHOP (rituximab + cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisolone) × 6 cycles. Cure in ~60–70%. Relapsed/refractory: salvage chemotherapy then autologous SCT if chemosensitive; CAR-T cell therapy (axicabtagene ciloleucel, lisocabtagene maraleucel) for ≥2 prior lines.

**Follicular lymphoma**: Watch and wait for asymptomatic low-burden disease. Rituximab monotherapy or R-CHOP when treatment needed. Not curative; median OS >20 years in modern series.

### Multiple Myeloma

**Not eligible for transplant**: VMP (bortezomib, melphalan, prednisolone) or Rd (lenalidomide, dexamethasone).

**Transplant-eligible**: VRd induction (bortezomib, lenalidomide, dexamethasone) → high-dose melphalan → autologous SCT → lenalidomide maintenance. Not curable but median OS now >7–10 years in modern trials.

**Supportive care**: Bisphosphonates (zoledronate) for all patients with bone disease — reduce skeletal events. Renal protection: aggressive hydration, avoid NSAIDs and nephrotoxins, treat hypercalcaemia. IVIG for recurrent infections due to hypogammaglobulinaemia.

## Complications

- **Tumour lysis syndrome (TLS)**: Massive cell death from chemotherapy → hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia, hyperuricaemia, AKI. Prevent with aggressive IV hydration and allopurinol (or rasburicase for high-risk). Commonest in Burkitt's lymphoma and ALL.
- **Febrile neutropaenia**: Neutropaenic sepsis is the most common cause of chemotherapy-related death — treat immediately with IV piperacillin-tazobactam within 60 minutes
- **Coagulopathy**: DIC in APL; factor X deficiency in AL amyloidosis; hyperviscosity in myeloma
- **Graft-versus-host disease (GvHD)**: After alloSCT — acute (skin, gut, liver) and chronic forms; treated with steroids and immunosuppression
- Secondary malignancies from treatment (secondary AML after alkylators, solid tumours after radiotherapy)

## Clinical Insight

APL (AML M3) is the one subtype of acute leukaemia where speed of specific treatment initiation is a matter of life and death. The coagulopathy of APL — from release of tissue factor-like procoagulants from abnormal promyelocytes — causes fatal haemorrhage within hours. As soon as APL is suspected on the blood film (hypergranular promyelocytes with bundles of Auer rods), start ATRA immediately — do not wait for cytogenetics. ATRA begins promyelocyte differentiation and halts the coagulopathy within days.

The Philadelphia chromosome transformed oncology twice — first as the molecular target that led to imatinib, and second as the proof of concept that targeted cancer therapy was possible. CML is now a model chronic disease managed with oral daily tablets. The lesson generalised to every other cancer with an identifiable molecular driver.

Watch and wait in follicular lymphoma and CLL is not inaction — it is an evidence-based decision that early treatment does not improve survival and exposes patients to unnecessary toxicity. The instinct to treat every cancer immediately is understandable but wrong in these contexts. The hardest clinical skill is sometimes to explain to a patient with lymphoma why you are not prescribing chemotherapy.
